# Paper 3 — Language Models are Few-Shot Learners

## Paper contribution

Brown et al. scale an autoregressive Transformer language model to **175 billion parameters** and systematically test whether task behavior can be induced through natural-language descriptions and in-context demonstrations without gradient updates. This paper is a P0 foundation for later general-purpose LLM agents because it establishes the practical idea that one pretrained model can adapt at inference time to many tasks through prompting.

## Model and evaluation design

- Eight model sizes are trained, ranging from **125M to 175B parameters**.
- GPT-3 is decoder-only/autoregressive and is evaluated without task-specific parameter updates.
- The paper distinguishes zero-shot, one-shot, and few-shot settings; few-shot typically uses as many demonstrations as fit in the **2048-token context window**, often roughly 10–100 examples depending on the task.
- Evaluation spans more than two dozen datasets plus synthetic/qualitative tasks, covering language modeling, closed-book QA, translation, Winograd-style reasoning, commonsense reasoning, reading comprehension, SuperGLUE/NLI, arithmetic, word manipulation, and novel-word use.

## Concrete results

Representative results demonstrate both capability and unevenness:

- **LAMBADA:** GPT-3 few-shot accuracy reaches **86.4%**, compared with 76.2% zero-shot and the cited previous state of the art of 68.0%.
- **TriviaQA:** closed-book GPT-3 reaches **64.3% zero-shot, 68.0% one-shot, and 71.2% few-shot**; the few-shot result exceeds the cited retrieval-augmented RAG result of 68.0 on that split.
- **WebQuestions:** 14.4% zero-shot, 25.3% one-shot, and **41.5% few-shot**, showing a much larger reliance on demonstrations.
- **Natural Questions:** 14.6% zero-shot, 23.0% one-shot, and **29.9% few-shot**, remaining below the cited task-specific T5+SSM result.
- Performance generally improves smoothly with model scale, and the gap between zero-, one-, and few-shot modes often grows with scale, suggesting increased in-context adaptation ability.

## Data-contamination audit

The paper explicitly treats benchmark contamination as a methodological threat because training data include large web corpora. It develops overlap checks, removes or flags severely contaminated results, and reports that contamination is limited for many benchmarks but material enough on some tasks to make unqualified leaderboard interpretation unsafe. This is especially relevant to web-agent evaluation, where benchmark pages and trajectories can also enter web-scale training corpora.

## Failure modes and limitations

The limitations section is substantive:

- Long-form generations can repeat, lose coherence, contradict themselves, or contain non sequiturs.
- GPT-3 remains weak on some forms of commonsense physics and on tasks requiring algorithmic/systematic generalization.
- Closed-book factual QA is highly dataset-dependent; strong TriviaQA performance does not transfer uniformly to Natural Questions or WebQuestions.
- In-context learning is less sample-efficient than human learning and remains sensitive to task phrasing/examples.
- The 2048-token context window constrains the amount of task evidence and memory available at inference time.
- Web-scale pretraining introduces contamination, social-bias, misuse, and energy-cost concerns.
- The paper demonstrates prompted task adaptation, not persistent memory, external tool use, verified provenance, browser grounding, or safe long-horizon action execution.

## Critical interpretation for LLM agents

GPT-3 is foundational because later agents exploit the same interface: task instructions and demonstrations are placed in context and the model produces a continuation that can be interpreted as a plan, tool call, action, or structured output. However, the paper also shows why this alone is insufficient for generalized web automation: task success varies sharply by distribution, factual knowledge is fallible, the context is finite, and the model has no built-in mechanism for external state verification.

## Synthesis statement

GPT-3 shows that scaling autoregressive Transformers yields substantial zero/one/few-shot task adaptation without weight updates—for example 86.4% few-shot LAMBADA and 71.2% few-shot TriviaQA—but the same paper documents uneven task transfer, finite-context constraints, contamination risk, coherence/factual failures, and societal costs, so it supports the prompting foundation of agents rather than reliable autonomous execution by itself.
