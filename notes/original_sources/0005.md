# Paper 5 — Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback

## Paper contribution

Ouyang et al. present **InstructGPT**, a practical reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback (RLHF) pipeline for aligning pretrained GPT-3 models with user intent. The paper is foundational for agent systems because modern agents rely on instruction-following models that must translate natural-language goals into useful, safe, and policy-constrained behavior.

## Training pipeline

The paper uses three stages:

1. **Supervised fine-tuning (SFT):** labelers write demonstrations of desired responses.
2. **Reward-model training:** labelers rank model outputs; a reward model is trained to predict those preferences.
3. **PPO optimization:** the language model is optimized against the reward model with a KL penalty; the main PPO-ptx variant also mixes a pretraining objective to reduce capability regressions.

Models use the GPT-3 architecture at **1.3B, 6B, and 175B parameters**. Human data include labeler-written prompts and prompts submitted through the OpenAI API, with privacy/safety filtering and held-out-customer evaluation.

## Concrete preference results

- Human evaluators prefer **175B InstructGPT over 175B GPT-3 85 ± 3% of the time** on the held-out API-prompt distribution.
- The same 175B InstructGPT is preferred to few-shot-prompted GPT-3 **71 ± 4%** of the time.
- A **1.3B InstructGPT** model is preferred to the 175B GPT-3 baseline despite having over 100× fewer parameters.
- The results show a stepwise improvement from raw GPT-3 → prompted GPT-3 → supervised fine-tuning → PPO/RLHF on the user-prompt distribution.

## Truthfulness, toxicity, and capability retention

- InstructGPT shows small but statistically significant improvements on TruthfulQA in human judgments for several PPO models.
- On RealToxicityPrompts, PPO instruction-following models are generally less toxic than non-instruction-following baselines **when instructed to be respectful**, but toxic prompts/instructions can still elicit toxic behavior.
- The paper explicitly reports **no clear reduction in bias** from the alignment procedure.
- Mixing pretraining data into PPO is used to reduce regressions on public NLP benchmarks such as SQuAD and DROP; increasing the KL coefficient alone does not fully remove those regressions.

## Important implementation evidence

- PPO training uses roughly **256k episodes** and about **31k unique prompts** after filtering/deduplication.
- The main experiments use a 6B reward model and 6B value function across policy sizes.
- Ablations show that the pretraining-mix coefficient, KL penalty, learning rate, training duration, and batch/minibatch sizes materially affect the alignment-performance trade-off.

## Limitations and failure modes

The paper is explicit that RLHF does not solve alignment:

- InstructGPT still makes simple mistakes and can produce false or misleading content.
- Behavior is shaped by the preferences and instructions given to a relatively small group of labelers, so the learned objective may not represent all users or contexts.
- Reward models can be exploited or overoptimized; preference improvement is not a guarantee of truthfulness or safety.
- The system can follow harmful or biased instructions and shows limited improvement on bias.
- PPO fine-tuning can regress capabilities on the pretrained distribution; the pretraining mix mitigates but does not eliminate this tension.
- The method is evaluated mainly on textual prompts/outputs, not autonomous tool execution or irreversible external actions.

## Relevance to LLM-based agents

This paper supports the **instruction-following and alignment substrate** of agents. It explains why an agent model can better follow natural-language goals than a raw pretrained LM, and why preference alignment must be treated as an imperfect control layer rather than a safety proof. In web automation, RLHF helps interpret user intent, but action authorization, state verification, provenance, and execution safeguards still need external mechanisms.

## Synthesis statement

InstructGPT demonstrates that human-feedback fine-tuning can make a much smaller model preferable to a 175B raw GPT-3 model and substantially improve instruction following, but the paper simultaneously documents residual falsehoods, bias, toxicity under harmful instructions, preference-distribution dependence, and capability regressions—limitations that remain directly relevant when such models are given agentic tools and external actions.
