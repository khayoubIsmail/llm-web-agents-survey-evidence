# Paper 11 — Least-to-Most Prompting Enables Complex Reasoning in Large Language Models

## Paper contribution

Zhou et al. propose **least-to-most prompting**, a two-stage prompting strategy for solving problems harder than the demonstrations in context. The method first decomposes a complex problem into simpler subproblems and then solves them sequentially, conditioning each later answer on earlier solved subproblems.

## Method

The procedure has two conceptual stages:

1. **Problem decomposition (top-down):** produce an ordered list of simpler subproblems.
2. **Sequential resolution (bottom-up):** solve the subproblems one by one, carrying forward previously generated answers as context.

Unlike ordinary chain-of-thought, which asks for a complete reasoning trace in one pass, least-to-most explicitly structures the task into reusable intermediate subproblems. The paper evaluates symbolic string manipulation, SCAN compositional generalization, and numerical reasoning on DROP/GSM8K.

## SCAN evidence

SCAN is used to test **length generalization**: test action sequences are longer than those used in demonstrations/training.

- The decomposition prompt uses **8 examples** and the mapping prompt uses **14 examples**.
- On the SCAN length split with code-davinci-002:
  - standard prompting: **16.7%**
  - chain-of-thought prompting: **16.2%**
  - least-to-most prompting: **99.7%**
- With text-davinci-002 the respective results are 6.0%, 0.0%, and **76.0%**.
- The paper reports the same near-perfect solving rate across other SCAN splits/full data with code-davinci-002.

The remaining 13 SCAN length-split errors are analyzed: six mis-handle “twice/thrice” after “around”; the others misinterpret “after” as “and.”

## DROP and GSM8K evidence

With code-davinci-002:

- DROP non-football numerical subset: CoT **74.77%** vs least-to-most **82.45%**.
- DROP football subset: CoT **59.56%** vs least-to-most **73.42%**.
- GSM8K: CoT **60.87%** vs least-to-most **62.39%**—a much smaller improvement than on DROP/SCAN.

This contrast is important: decomposition is highly effective when the task admits a clear decomposition structure, but it is not uniformly transformative across all reasoning problems.

## Symbolic-manipulation evidence

For last-letter concatenation and related synthetic tasks, least-to-most generalizes from shorter demonstration lists to longer test lists. The decomposition process can reach 100% decomposition accuracy in the studied setup. The paper also tests expansion of intermediate Python expressions via prompting and reports **99.7%** accuracy on a random sample of 1,000 expressions, showing that the method need not rely strictly on an external Python executor for that final expansion.

## Limitations and boundaries

- The exceptional SCAN/last-letter results depend on **decomposition being relatively straightforward**, a qualification stated by the authors.
- Performance depends on the quality of the decomposition prompt and the model's ability to solve each subproblem; decomposition errors can propagate downstream.
- Some tasks show only modest benefit (e.g., GSM8K relative to CoT), so the method should not be characterized as a universal reasoning improvement.
- The prompting schemes are task-designed; they do not automatically discover arbitrary domain-specific decomposition operators.
- The experiments do not test interactive state changes, browser grounding, tool failure recovery, provenance, or permission constraints.

## Relevance to LLM agents

Least-to-most prompting provides a direct conceptual bridge to **hierarchical agent planning**: decompose a long goal into tractable subgoals and execute/resolve them sequentially. For generalized web automation, that can map to locating a source, applying filters, traversing pages, extracting records, validating fields, and assembling a final dataset. But a practical agent must ground each subgoal in live environment state and recover when an earlier step changes or fails.

## Synthesis statement

Least-to-most prompting turns a difficult task into an ordered sequence of simpler subproblems and achieves 99.7% on SCAN's length split versus roughly 16% for standard/CoT prompting with code-davinci-002; this is strong evidence for hierarchical decomposition in agents, but gains depend on decomposability and errors can propagate across the subgoal chain.
