# Visual Instruction Tuning

## Paper-specific synthesis

### Problem and contribution

Liu et al. extend text instruction tuning into the image-language setting and introduce **LLaVA (Large Language and Vision Assistant)**. The paper contributes three connected pieces: a GPT-4-assisted method for creating visual instruction-following data, an end-to-end multimodal assistant that connects a frozen CLIP vision encoder to Vicuna, and LLaVA-Bench for evaluating visual instruction following.

### Data construction

Because image-instruction-response triples were scarce, the authors convert existing image-text annotations into instruction-following conversations. A text-only GPT-4 receives symbolic descriptions of an image—captions and object bounding boxes—and generates three response types: conversation, detailed description, and complex reasoning. The resulting **LLaVA-Instruct-158K** corpus contains **158K unique language-image instruction samples: 58K conversations, 23K detailed descriptions, and 77K complex-reasoning examples**.

For feature alignment, CC3M is filtered to approximately **595K image-text pairs**. This alignment stage trains only a projection from CLIP visual features into the LLM embedding space.

### Architecture and training

LLaVA uses a **CLIP ViT-L/14** visual encoder, a trainable linear projection, and Vicuna as the language model. The visual encoder stays frozen. Training has two stages:

1. **Feature alignment:** train the projection on CC-595K while freezing both CLIP and the LLM.
2. **Instruction tuning:** update the projection and Vicuna on LLaVA-Instruct-158K while continuing to freeze the vision encoder.

The reported setup uses 8 A100 GPUs; pre-training on CC-595K takes about 4 hours, instruction fine-tuning about 10 hours, and ScienceQA fine-tuning about 4 hours.

### Concrete evaluation results

- On the synthetic multimodal instruction-following evaluation, full-data LLaVA obtains **85.1% relative score** versus the text-only GPT-4 reference, with category scores of **83.1 conversation, 75.3 detailed description, and 96.5 complex reasoning**.
- LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild) contains **24 images and 60 questions**. LLaVA outperforms BLIP-2 by **29%** and OpenFlamingo by **48%**, while reaching **81.7%** of the GPT-4 reference on complex-reasoning questions and **67.3% overall**.
- On ScienceQA, LLaVA alone reaches **90.92% accuracy**. Combining LLaVA with text-only GPT-4 as a judge reaches **92.53%**, exceeding the cited MM-CoT Large result of 91.68%.
- Removing the pre-training/alignment stage drops ScienceQA performance from 90.92% to **85.81% (-5.11 points)**.
- Using the CLIP final-layer features gives **89.96% (-0.96)**, and reducing Vicuna from 13B to 7B gives **89.84% (-1.08)**.

### Failure modes and limitations

The paper explicitly shows that visual instruction following is constrained by perception and grounding. On an in-the-wild refrigerator example, LLaVA incorrectly infers strawberry-flavored yogurt from the co-occurrence of strawberries and yogurt, illustrating a failure to integrate image parts into coherent scene semantics. The benchmark also exposes difficulties with high-resolution details, multilingual text, broad world knowledge, and cases that may require external multimodal retrieval.

The broader-impact section notes hallucination, malicious inputs, inherited data/model biases, energy consumption, and the difficulty of evaluating open-ended multimodal responses. A further methodological limitation is that GPT-4 serves both as data generator and evaluation judge in important parts of the pipeline, which can introduce model-specific evaluation bias.

### Relevance to generalized Web agents

LLaVA provides direct foundational evidence for **multimodal perception and instruction grounding**. Web agents that rely on screenshots rather than DOM/AX representations need exactly this type of joint visual-language interface. The paper also demonstrates why screenshot-only grounding remains fragile: spatial details, high-resolution text, compositional scene semantics, and hallucination can fail even when language reasoning is strong.

It does not evaluate browser navigation, DOM actions, provenance, schema-guided extraction, or long-horizon Web task completion, so it should support the survey's multimodal-observation layer rather than claims about full Web-agent competence.

### One-sentence synthesis

Visual Instruction Tuning shows that a frozen vision encoder can be aligned with an instruction-tuned LLM using generated multimodal data to obtain strong visual instruction following, but its own benchmark examples reveal perception, high-resolution grounding, and hallucination failures that remain critical for screenshot-driven Web agents.
