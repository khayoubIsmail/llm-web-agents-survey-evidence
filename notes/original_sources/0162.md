# OS Agents: A Survey on MLLM-based Agents for General Computing Devices Use

## Paper-specific synthesis

### Scope and definition

Hu et al. define **OS Agents** as (multi-modal) LLM-based agents that operate computers, phones and browsers through interfaces supplied by an operating system, including graphical and command-line interfaces. The survey deliberately treats computer use as an agent problem composed of environment state, an observation space and an action space rather than as ordinary text generation.

### Taxonomy

The survey organizes OS-agent capability around three core functions:

- **Understanding:** interpreting instructions and the current device/interface state.
- **Planning:** decomposing goals and maintaining a multi-step course of action.
- **Grounding:** mapping semantic decisions to concrete interface elements/actions.

Construction approaches are then divided into domain-specific foundation models and agent frameworks. The survey compares observation representations such as screenshots, accessibility/structured UI information and text, as well as action spaces ranging from low-level mouse/keyboard interaction to structured APIs.

### Evaluation synthesis

The benchmark review spans computers, smartphones and browser environments and emphasizes that benchmark success is strongly coupled to observation/action interfaces. Static perception accuracy is insufficient: OS agents must maintain state across interactions and recover from changed screens or failed actions.

### Relevance to generalized Web agents

This is highly relevant secondary evidence because browser agents are explicitly included within the OS-agent scope. Its separation of **observation, understanding, planning, grounding and action** maps directly to the survey's capability stack. It also provides a useful bridge between Web-agent research and broader computer-use/GUI-agent literature.

### Challenges and limitations

The survey highlights safety/privacy, personalization, robustness/generalization and self-evolution as open problems. Real device actions can expose credentials or create irreversible effects; screenshots can contain sensitive information; and benchmark environments do not fully capture changing real applications. As a survey, it should support taxonomy and cross-benchmark synthesis rather than quantitative superiority claims about individual systems.

### Evidence locations

OS-agent definition and components: fundamentals section. Understanding/planning/grounding: capability section. Foundation models/frameworks: construction sections. Benchmark organization: evaluation section. Safety/privacy/personalization/self-evolution: challenge/future-work section.

### One-sentence synthesis

The OS Agents survey frames computer, phone and browser automation around environment observations, planning, grounding and actions, providing a directly applicable taxonomy for Web agents while emphasizing unresolved safety, privacy and generalization problems.
