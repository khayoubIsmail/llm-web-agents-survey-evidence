# From Language to Action: A Review of Large Language Models as Autonomous Agents and Tool Users

## Paper-specific synthesis

### Review scope and selection

Chowa et al. review the transition from LLMs as passive language systems to autonomous agents and tool users through **seven research questions**. Their stated review scope is deliberately selective: papers from **2023–2025** in A*/A-ranked conferences and Q1 journals. The final synthesis includes architectural design principles, tool integration, cognitive mechanisms, adaptation methods and evaluation.

### Architecture and capability synthesis

The review separates **single-agent and multi-agent systems** and analyzes how external tools extend the model beyond parametric knowledge. Reasoning, planning and memory are treated as interacting cognitive mechanisms rather than isolated prompting tricks. Prompting and fine-tuning are compared as ways to alter agent behavior, while retrieval, APIs and tools provide access to external state/capabilities.

### Evaluation evidence

A notable contribution is an inventory and analysis of **68 publicly available datasets/benchmarks** used to assess LLM-agent behavior. The paper argues that current evaluation is fragmented across task domains and that final-answer/task-success metrics alone are insufficient for properties such as verifiable reasoning, self-improvement and personalization.

### Identified gaps and directions

The review highlights verifiable reasoning, self-improvement and personalization as major unresolved themes and concludes with **ten future research directions**. Across these directions, recurrent concerns are reliable planning, trustworthy tool execution, efficient adaptation, robust evaluation and the alignment of agent actions with user/environment constraints.

### Relevance to generalized Web automation

This is useful secondary evidence for linking Web-agent architectures to the broader agent/tool-use literature. The 68-benchmark inventory is particularly useful for evaluation context, while the single-/multi-agent and cognitive/tool taxonomies help position browser agents. It should not replace primary Web benchmark/system papers for exact empirical claims.

### Limitations

The review's venue/date inclusion rule intentionally omits lower-ranked, workshop and pre-2023 work, so it is not a complete historical census. The 68 datasets span heterogeneous capabilities and cannot be treated as directly comparable. As with other broad reviews, conclusions about agent effectiveness are mediated by the primary studies' differing models, prompts and evaluation protocols.

### Evidence locations

Review protocol/seven RQs: methods and introduction. Agent architecture/tool integration: central taxonomy sections. Reasoning/planning/memory and adaptation: cognitive/training sections. Benchmark inventory: evaluation section/tables. Findings and ten directions: discussion/future-work sections.

### One-sentence synthesis

This open-access review structures recent autonomous-agent research through seven questions and 68 evaluation datasets, connecting architecture, tools, reasoning, planning and memory while emphasizing verifiability, self-improvement and personalization as unresolved problems.
