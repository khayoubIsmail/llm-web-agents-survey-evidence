# A Survey on the Optimization of Large Language Model-based Agents

## Paper-specific synthesis

### Scope and principal taxonomy

Du et al. treat **LLM-agent optimization** as a distinct topic rather than ordinary base-model optimization. Their central taxonomy divides approaches into **parameter-driven** and **parameter-free** optimization.

Parameter-driven approaches include conventional fine-tuning, reinforcement-learning-based optimization and hybrid strategies. The survey decomposes these further into trajectory/data construction, fine-tuning techniques, reward-function design, preference alignment and optimization algorithms. Parameter-free approaches improve the running agent without updating its weights, including historical-experience integration, feedback/reflection, tool use, retrieval and multi-agent collaboration.

### Systems and evaluation perspective

The paper connects optimization methods to the practical agent stack: planning, memory, tool invocation and execution control, and discusses open-source frameworks such as LangChain and AutoGen as reusable infrastructure. It also organizes datasets used both to train/tune agents and to evaluate action, reasoning and interaction capabilities across application domains.

A useful conceptual distinction is that optimizing a general LLM objective does not necessarily optimize long-term planning, dynamic environmental interaction or complex decision making. Agent optimization therefore needs trajectory- and environment-aware objectives rather than assuming improvements on static language benchmarks will transfer automatically.

### Relevance to generalized Web agents

For Web automation, this taxonomy separates two intervention levels clearly. An agent can be improved offline by fine-tuning/RL on browser trajectories, or online without weight changes by retrieval, experience memory, reflection, tool orchestration and multi-agent verification. This helps structure comparisons that would otherwise mix fundamentally different optimization costs and supervision assumptions.

### Challenges and limitations

The survey highlights data quality/scarcity for realistic interaction trajectories, reward/evaluator reliability, generalization, efficiency and safety. Parameter-driven methods can be expensive and overfit benchmark environments; parameter-free methods can add inference cost and depend on potentially unreliable memories, feedback or tools. Because it is a survey of heterogeneous studies, it cannot establish one universally best optimization strategy.

### One-sentence synthesis

The ACM CSUR survey distinguishes parameter-driven agent optimization from inference-time parameter-free improvement, providing a useful framework for comparing trajectory fine-tuning/RL against memory, feedback, retrieval and tool-based optimization in Web agents.
