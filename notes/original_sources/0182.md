## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

The paper argues that text-only pretraining leaves language models weak on embodied facts such as object permanence, state tracking, and feasible household plans. It proposes E2WM, a training paradigm that gathers embodied experience from the VirtualHome world model and uses those trajectories to fine-tune a language model while attempting to preserve its general language competence.

### Method and evaluation

An embodied agent collects two kinds of experience in VirtualHome: goal-directed plans and random exploration trajectories. These experiences train GPT-Neo, GPT-J, OPT, and LLaMA variants using LoRA, with elastic-weight consolidation intended to limit catastrophic forgetting. Evaluation spans 18 downstream tasks covering plan generation, activity and object-state reasoning, plus relevant bAbI tasks. The paper reports a 64.28% average improvement over the base models; smaller enhanced models sometimes match or exceed much larger general models. The detailed results also show large task-specific variation, so the average should not be read as uniform performance.

### Limitations and critical assessment

VirtualHome is a simulator with a fixed ontology and executable action grammar. Its experience distribution is cleaner than either the physical world or the web, and simulator correctness does not guarantee transfer. Some comparisons also mix models of different sizes and access conditions. EWC and LoRA reduce forgetting and training cost but do not eliminate domain overfitting. The study demonstrates useful transfer from a world model, not general embodied understanding.

### Relevance to the survey

The work supports treating a world model as an experience generator and knowledge source for agents. For web automation, an analogous approach could use browser simulators or site models, while validating transfer against live pages and unseen workflows.
