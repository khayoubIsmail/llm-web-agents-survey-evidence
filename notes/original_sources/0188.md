# AlphaZero-Like Tree-Search can Guide Large Language Model Decoding and Training

## Research problem and contribution

TS-LLM asks how tree search can assist LLM reasoning when a prompted LLM is not already a trustworthy value function and when trajectories are long. It adapts the AlphaZero pattern: search is guided by learned policy, value, and outcome-reward components, and search-improved trajectories can in turn train those components.

## Method and evaluation

The framework supports both inference-time decoding and iterative policy improvement. It expands token- or step-level reasoning trees, evaluates partial trajectories, retains successful paths, distils them into the policy, and retrains value/reward models. Experiments span GSM8K, Game24, PrOntoQA, a synthetic RLHF task, and chess endgames, deliberately varying search depth and branching. Comparisons include chain-of-thought sampling and prior tree-search methods. The paper reports substantial gains in selected settings; one ablation increases performance from 43.8 to 80.7 when moving to the more effective token-level search configuration.

## Limitations and critical assessment

Search quality depends on learned evaluators and becomes expensive as depth and branching grow. Benchmark rewards are much cleaner than real web-task success criteria, and training the auxiliary models requires task data. The approach therefore shows that search plus learned evaluation can beat prompting-only search, but not that it is economical or reliable for open-ended environments.

## Relevance to the survey

Use for planning and verifier-guided search. A web agent can benefit from search only when intermediate states can be scored credibly; otherwise more branches amplify cost without improving decisions.
