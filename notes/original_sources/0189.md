# AutoAgents: A Framework for Automatic Agent Generation

## Research problem and contribution

AutoAgents replaces a fixed multi-agent cast with task-conditioned team construction. Given a task, it generates specialized roles, develops a plan, coordinates their responses, and assigns an observer to critique both the plan and the resulting answer. The contribution is therefore orchestration logic for adapting the team structure, not a new base model.

## Method and evaluation

The workflow separates drafting from execution and permits repeated individual and collaborative refinement. Experiments use GPT-4 with deterministic decoding. Quantitative evaluation covers open-ended questions drawn from MT-Bench and a creative-writing task, with comparisons to single-model and multi-agent baselines; a software-development case study illustrates a longer workflow. The paper reports better judged coherence and accuracy, including an approximately 10% improvement in the highlighted aggregate comparison. Ablation results show a drop when key collaboration components are removed.

## Limitations and critical assessment

The study relies heavily on one proprietary backbone and model-based or subjective evaluation. Gains may reflect extra inference calls or judge preference rather than role specialization itself. Team generation also increases latency and makes failure attribution difficult. The software case study demonstrates feasibility, not controlled generalization.

## Relevance to the survey

AutoAgents supports dynamic role allocation in agent architectures. For web automation, dynamically adding a navigator, verifier, or extractor can be useful, but the survey should separate benefits of specialization from benefits caused simply by spending more tokens.
