# Corex: Pushing the Boundaries of Complex Reasoning through Multi-Model Collaboration

## Research problem and contribution

Corex investigates whether multiple heterogeneous LLMs can jointly overcome errors produced by a single model. It contributes three general collaboration patterns—Discuss, Review, and Retrieve—that turn models into cooperating reasoning agents without task-specific training.

## Method and evaluation

Discuss elicits multiple reasoning paths and reconciles them; Review assigns models to inspect and revise candidates; Retrieve uses another model's output as additional knowledge. The experiments cover 18 tasks across arithmetic, commonsense, symbolic reasoning, and semi-structured question answering. Main comparisons use GPT-3.5-Turbo, with additional analyses using GPT-4, Claude, and LLaMA-2-Chat. Baselines include chain-of-thought, self-consistency, complexity-based consistency, and program-aided reasoning. The paper reports broad gains, such as more than six percentage points over ComplexCoT on StrategyQA, while also analyzing cross-model synergies and cost.

## Limitations and critical assessment

The collaboration protocols add calls and can correlate errors when agents share training data or model families. Most tasks have short, verifiable answers and do not represent long-horizon action. Results using proprietary systems can also change with model versions. Corex demonstrates ensemble-like benefits but does not isolate all gains from extra sampling and compute.

## Relevance to the survey

Use as evidence for multi-model critique and knowledge exchange. In web agents, independent retrieval and verification roles are promising only if diversity and added cost are measured explicitly.
