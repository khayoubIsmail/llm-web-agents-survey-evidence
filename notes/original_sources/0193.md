# LLaMA-Rider: Spurring Large Language Models to Explore the Open World

## Research problem and contribution

LLaMA-Rider studies continual adaptation in an open environment rather than one-shot prompting. It contributes a feedback–revision loop that lets an LLM choose revision actions after failures and adds subtask relabeling so collected experience teaches reusable combinations of skills.

## Method and evaluation

The agent uses a language-model planner over a retrieved set of Minecraft skills. Environmental feedback triggers multi-round plan revision, while successful and failed exploration episodes supply approximately 1.3k fine-tuning instances. Subtask labels preserve consistency between the intended subgoal and chosen skill. Evaluation uses 30 Plan4MC tasks in MineDojo, divided into log-, cobblestone-, and mob-related groups with an average minimum plan length of 11.5 steps. Results compare exploration efficiency, task success, fine-tuned versus prompted models, and design ablations.

## Limitations and critical assessment

The action space is built from 55 pre-trained skills and noun/embedding retrieval, so the system explores combinations of an engineered repertoire rather than arbitrary Minecraft behavior. Skill failures and simulator-specific feedback confound planner quality. The small adaptation set is encouraging, but the study does not establish transfer to a new environment or interface.

## Relevance to the survey

Use for experience accumulation and failure-driven revision. Web agents likewise need to convert execution feedback into reusable skills, but should distinguish planner learning from improvements in the underlying action library.
