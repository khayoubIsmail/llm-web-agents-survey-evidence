# Automating the Generation of Prompts for LLM-based Action Choice in PDDL Planning

## Research problem and contribution

This paper removes a manual bottleneck in evaluating LLM planning: converting PDDL domains into natural-language prompts. It uses an LLM to generate domain descriptions automatically and evaluates both complete-plan generation and stepwise action choice across a broader set of planning domains.

## Method and evaluation

Predicate and action definitions from PDDL are translated into natural-language descriptions, combined with state/goal information and few-shot examples, and supplied to an LLM. The authors compare automatically generated descriptions with manual descriptions, direct PDDL, and simpler template-based language. Automatic descriptions achieve performance similar to manual prompts and generally improve over raw PDDL or templates, enabling larger experiments. However, classical symbolic planners remain substantially more reliable, particularly as plans become difficult or long.

## Limitations and critical assessment

The generation pipeline can introduce semantic errors into the domain description, and valid-looking language does not guarantee preservation of PDDL preconditions and effects. Standard planning domains are closed and fully observable; live web tasks add hidden state, stochasticity, and changing interfaces. The results therefore support automated interface translation, not the claim that LLMs replace planners.

## Relevance to the survey

This is useful evidence for translating formal action schemas into model-readable instructions and for hybrid LLM–symbolic planning. Web-agent evaluations should retain an executable checker rather than judge plans only by textual plausibility.
