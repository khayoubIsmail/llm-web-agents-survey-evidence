# A Survey on LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems: Workflow, Infrastructure, and Challenges

## Research problem and contribution

This survey proposes a workflow-centered account of LLM-based multi-agent systems. It organizes systems into five components—profile, perception, self-action, mutual interaction, and evolution—and separates two broad application families: collaborative problem solving and simulation of social or environmental processes.

## Method and findings

The authors synthesize prior systems and benchmarks rather than run a new controlled experiment. Their framework makes otherwise inconsistent role labels comparable by asking what information an agent receives, how it acts alone, how agents communicate, and whether the team changes through reflection or learning. The application tables also record environment, backbone, feedback, and evaluation choices. The review identifies recurring challenges in coordination, communication quality, scalability, evaluation, hallucination, and safety.

## Limitations and critical assessment

The taxonomy is descriptive and depends on a fast-moving literature; it does not establish which component causally improves performance. Included studies differ sharply in model access, token budget, task difficulty, and judge design, so their scores cannot be compared as a league table. The survey's broad claim that multi-agent systems may lead toward general intelligence is motivational rather than empirically demonstrated.

## Relevance to the survey

Use the five-component structure as a cross-check when describing multi-agent web architectures, especially to distinguish internal action from inter-agent interaction and genuine learning from within-episode reflection.
