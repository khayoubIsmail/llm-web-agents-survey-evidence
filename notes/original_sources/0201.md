# Position: Towards Unified Alignment Between Agents, Humans, and Environment

## Research problem and contribution

UA² is a position paper arguing that agent alignment has three simultaneous targets: human intentions, environmental dynamics, and the agent's own constraints such as time and monetary budgets. Its practical contribution is a modified WebShop setting that exposes all three instead of reducing alignment to task reward.

## Method and evaluation

The retrofit adds user profiles to represent preferences, personalized reranking to make environment responses conditional, and explicit runtime/cost statistics. A proof-of-concept agent is compared with ReAct, self-consistency, Reflexion, and LATS using success/reward, human-intention and environment-dynamics gaps, time, and money. No method dominates every metric: search/reflection baselines can lead on success or reward, while the proposed design yields the strongest reported balance under the unified measure.

## Limitations and critical assessment

The unified score necessarily embeds value judgments about how to weight task quality, preference fit, adaptation, latency, and cost. The WebShop modifications are a useful testbed but remain simulated, and the chosen profiles/reranker do not span real user ambiguity. This is evidence for multidimensional evaluation, not a settled alignment objective.

## Relevance to the survey

The paper directly supports evaluating web agents beyond success rate. A defensible benchmark should report user-intent satisfaction, adaptation to live state, and resource constraints separately before any aggregate score.
