# Can LLM-Augmented Autonomous Agents Cooperate? An Evaluation of Their Cooperative Capabilities Through Melting Pot

## Research problem and contribution

This study asks whether LLM-augmented agents cooperate effectively in unfamiliar social dilemmas. It contributes a textual abstraction layer for DeepMind's Melting Pot environments and a reusable agent architecture with short- and long-term memory plus perception, planning, reflection, and action modules.

## Method and evaluation

Experiments use the Commons Harvest scenario, where three LLM agents and two learned bots share a renewable apple resource. The agent chooses high-level actions such as movement, exploration, waiting, or immobilizing another player. GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 configurations are evaluated under several social conditions using cooperation-oriented measures rather than individual reward alone. The qualitative and quantitative pattern is mixed: agents often show cooperative intent but fail to coordinate it reliably when conditions or other actors are unfamiliar.

## Limitations and critical assessment

One gridworld scenario cannot establish general cooperation, and converting the environment to text changes the information and action problem. Model behavior may also be sensitive to prompting and model-version drift. The work is best read as a diagnostic showing a gap between cooperative language and effective joint policy, not as a ranking of LLMs' social intelligence.

## Relevance to the survey

For multi-agent web systems, the paper warns that role descriptions and prosocial dialogue do not prove coordination. Evaluation must measure resource conflicts, joint outcomes, and behavior under unfamiliar partners.
