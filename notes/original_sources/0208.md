# Language Guided Exploration for RL Agents in Text Environments

## Research problem and contribution

Language Guided Exploration (LGE) reduces the combinatorial action space faced by reinforcement-learning agents in sparse-reward text environments. A pretrained language “Guide” ranks feasible actions from the task and state, while an RL “Explorer” learns among that reduced set.

## Method and evaluation

The Guide is trained contrastively from supervised demonstrations so compatible task–action pairs score above incompatible ones. During interaction, its candidates constrain or bias the Explorer's exploration. ScienceWorld experiments compare LGE with vanilla RL, behavior cloning, and a Text Decision Transformer across scientific tasks. The Guide's reported average precision is about 0.68, and LGE improves mean task performance in the studied configurations despite imperfect filtering.

## Limitations and critical assessment

The study is limited to English ScienceWorld and requires labeled demonstrations for the Guide. If the Guide excludes a necessary action, the RL policy cannot recover; thus action pruning trades exploration efficiency for coverage risk. The fixed text action interface also understates perception and grounding problems on the web.

## Relevance to the survey

LGE provides evidence for language-informed action-space reduction. A web agent can rank DOM actions or tools, but should preserve an escape path when the language model's shortlist is wrong.
