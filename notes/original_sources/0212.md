# Toward Self-Improvement of LLMs via Imagination, Searching, and Criticizing

## Research problem and contribution

AlphaLLM creates a self-improvement loop around Monte Carlo tree search without new human annotations. It combines prompt synthesis, an MCTS variant adapted to large language action spaces, and three critic signals: a value function, a process reward model, and an outcome reward model.

## Method and evaluation

Search generates candidate reasoning trajectories; critics score intermediate and final states; successful trajectories then improve the policy. Experiments use GSM8K and a computationally restricted subset of MATH, with Llama-2-70B and WizardMath-70B policies and comparisons to proprietary and open models. Reported performance rises from 57.8 to 92.0 on GSM8K and from 20.7 to 51.0 on MATH across the full self-improvement configuration described by the authors. Ablations show smaller gains from individual critics and quantify rollout cost; the ηMCTS MATH result reaches 45.4 with 148 rollouts in one comparison.

## Limitations and critical assessment

Mathematics supplies unusually clear final-answer rewards. Subjective or delayed web-task outcomes make critic training harder, and the rollout budget is large. The comparison to GPT-4 is task-specific and should not be generalized to overall model capability. Self-generated trajectories can also amplify critic blind spots.

## Relevance to the survey

Use for self-improvement through search and learned verification, while emphasizing that reliable reward signals and rollout cost determine whether the method transfers to agents.
