# PRACT: Optimizing Principled Reasoning and Acting of LLM Agent

## Research problem and contribution

PRAct learns explicit action principles from successful and failed trajectories. A reflector produces textual “gradients” about the current principles, and Reflective Principle Optimization updates them either per trajectory or in batches before they guide later reasoning and action.

## Method and evaluation

The policy receives context plus a compact set of principles tailored to operations such as search or click. After execution, RPO-Traj updates principles from an individual trace, while RPO-Batch consolidates feedback across traces. Tests cover WebShop plus academic, movie, and weather tool environments with GPT-3.5-Turbo and GPT-4-Turbo. PRAct variants outperform Act, ReAct, Reflexion, and ExpeL across the displayed tasks; batch optimization is generally stronger than trajectory-wise updates.

## Limitations and critical assessment

Principles are natural-language artifacts produced and interpreted by LLMs, so they can be vague, contradictory, or overfit to common failures. The experiments use a small family of tool environments and proprietary backbones. Improvements may partly arise from additional reflection calls, and principles need versioning when interfaces change.

## Relevance to the survey

PRAct is useful for converting trajectory evidence into concise policy constraints. For web agents, such principles should cite the failures that motivated them and be invalidated when site behavior changes.
