# ACC-Collab: An Actor-Critic Approach to Multi-Agent LLM Collaboration

## Research problem and contribution

ACC-Collab treats collaboration as a learned behavior rather than assuming instruction-tuned models already know how to deliberate. It trains an actor and critic jointly using an actor–critic-style objective and collaborative trajectories.

## Method and evaluation

The actor proposes answers and revisions; the critic supplies assessments across up to five deliberation rounds. Training uses positive/negative collaborative trajectories, with guided trajectories addressing weak initial policies. Evaluation covers BoolQ, MMLU, BBH, SCIQ, and ARC using Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct, and Gemma-2-2B-Instruct. Baselines include Society of Minds, Persona, SFT, DebateTune, and DebateGPT. ACC-Collab achieves the strongest or near-strongest average accuracy in most reported cells with 95% confidence intervals; a second training round sometimes hurts, motivating held-out selection.

## Limitations and critical assessment

Collecting high-quality positive and negative trajectories is compute intensive and particularly difficult when the base team rarely succeeds. The tasks are mostly static QA, so learned deliberation may not transfer to action coordination. Five rounds also confound training quality with inference budget.

## Relevance to the survey

The paper supports explicitly training critic–actor communication. For web systems, collaboration quality should be evaluated on shared state and coordinated actions, not only final-answer accuracy.
