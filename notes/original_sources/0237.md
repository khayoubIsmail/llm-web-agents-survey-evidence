# OPTIMA: Optimizing Effectiveness and Efficiency for LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems

## Research problem and contribution

OPTIMA jointly optimizes multi-agent task quality and communication efficiency. It iterates through generate, rank, select, and train stages, with a reward combining task performance, token use, and communication readability. SFT, DPO, and a hybrid are compared.

## Method and evaluation

For preference-data generation, conversation turns are treated as nodes in an MCTS-inspired search over interaction paths. Selected traces train the collaborating agents through iterative SFT and/or DPO. Evaluation includes information-asymmetric QA and complex reasoning, with in- and out-of-distribution tests. The paper reports up to a 90% reduction in inference tokens and as much as 2.8× task performance relative to selected baselines; on 2WikiMultiHopQA the hybrid improves F1 by 38.3% in the highlighted comparison.

## Limitations and critical assessment

Experiments primarily use two-agent systems, so the method's scaling behavior is unresolved. Results depend on the chosen scalar reward weights, which can trade readability or task quality against token count. Iterative data generation and training shift cost from inference to optimization and may be substantial.

## Relevance to the survey

OPTIMA is important evidence that multi-agent comparisons must control communication budget. Its reward formulation is a useful model for reporting quality–cost Pareto fronts in web agents.
