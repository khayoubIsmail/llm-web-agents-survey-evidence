### Key finding / main claim

Foundation agents can be extended toward more general computer-use tasks, but robust control remains difficult.

For S4, the important question is not only whether the method works on its benchmark, but **which part of web/GUI agency it improves**:

- perception and grounding,
- planning and reasoning,
- action execution,
- API/tool use,
- memory/state tracking,
- recovery and verification,
- or deployment/evaluation.

---

### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

- **Architecture:** Cradle's GCC agent combines information gathering, self-reflection, task inference, skill curation, action planning and memory while interacting through screenshots and keyboard/mouse inputs.
- **Breadth:** the system is evaluated on **four commercial games plus five software applications**, with additional OSWorld testing.
- **Long horizon:** case studies include **40-minute Red Dead Redemption 2 missions**, growing a Cities: Skylines city beyond **1,000 residents**, and Dealer's Life scenarios with high weekly profit.
- **Transfer claim:** the same scaffolding is applied across games and productivity software without application-specific API access.
- **Survey implication:** general computer control needs persistent skill/memory abstractions in addition to multimodal action selection.

#### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

Case-study success is difficult to compare with standardized benchmark success, and screenshot/keyboard control remains slow and error-prone. Commercial game/software versions can change and complicate exact replication.

### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** Under-review/preprint status.
- **Limitation 2:** General computer control is broader than web automation and may include many environment-specific assumptions.
- **Limitation 3:** Reliability, safety, and reproducibility remain open concerns.

General thesis-level limitation:

```text
Improving one agent component is not enough for generalized web automation.
A robust web agent still needs grounding, planning, execution, verification, recovery, and safety.
```
