### Key finding / main claim

LLMs transform phone automation by improving intent understanding, multimodal perception, and decision-making, but challenges remain in data diversity, on-device efficiency, adaptation, and security.

For S4, the important question is not only whether the method works on its benchmark, but **which part of web/GUI agency it improves**:

- perception and grounding,
- planning and reasoning,
- action execution,
- API/tool use,
- memory/state tracking,
- recovery and verification,
- or deployment/evaluation.

---

### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

- **Taxonomy:** phone agents are categorized into direct single-agent policies, multi-agent systems and explicit plan-act architectures.
- **Training:** the survey distinguishes prompting-only approaches from supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning and trajectory/self-improvement methods.
- **Data bottleneck:** limited diverse high-quality mobile trajectories remain a central training constraint.
- **Deployment:** on-device latency/compute, personalization, permission/security and benchmark standardization are identified as major unresolved concerns.
- **Reliability:** the survey emphasizes that successful UI grounding is necessary but insufficient without planning, recovery and safe state manipulation.

#### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

This is synthesis evidence rather than a new experimental benchmark, and mobile-agent system rankings are highly sensitive to fast-changing model/app versions.

---

### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** Survey, not primary method.
- **Limitation 2:** Phone-specific focus may not cover all web/desktop agents in depth.
- **Limitation 3:** Recent field changes may quickly make some taxonomy parts outdated.

General thesis-level limitation:

```text
Improving one agent component is not enough for generalized web automation.
A robust web agent still needs grounding, planning, execution, verification, recovery, and safety.
```
