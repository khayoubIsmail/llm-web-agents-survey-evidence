### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

- **Benchmark:** A3 evaluates agents on **201 tasks across 21 widely used third-party Android apps** in real online mobile conditions.
- **Task realism:** scenarios include live information retrieval and operational tasks rather than only offline/static app snapshots.
- **Evaluation:** the framework combines executable/function checks with LLM-based business-level assessment; the archival update strengthens this with essential-state procedural evaluation.
- **Finding:** GUI-specialized agents outperform generalist commercial models on many task categories, showing that generic multimodal reasoning is not sufficient for reliable mobile control.
- **Reproducibility issue addressed:** essential states provide intermediate evidence when final-state-only evaluation is ambiguous.

#### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

Live third-party apps change frequently and can require accounts/network access. LLM-based evaluation must be calibrated, and essential-state definitions still require task-specific authoring.

### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** Mobile UI tasks may not generalize directly to desktop web automation.
- **Limitation 2:** Benchmarks can become stale as apps update their interfaces.
- **Limitation 3:** Success depends heavily on observation format, action space, and evaluation rules.
