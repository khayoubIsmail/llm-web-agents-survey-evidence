# HEAP- HIERARCHICAL POLICIES FOR

## Research problem and contribution

The work registered under its earlier HEAP title was published as SteP: Stacked LLM Policies for Web Actions. SteP decomposes web behavior into reusable policies but avoids a fixed hierarchy: the active policy can invoke another policy, act on the environment, or return control, with a stack recording the current call chain and isolating local histories.

## Method and evaluation

SteP augments the web-agent state with a stack of policies and adds invoke and return actions to normal browser actions. A library of 14 policies is manually designed for WebArena, and smaller task-specific libraries are used for MiniWoB++ and an AirlineCRM simulator. Evaluation covers all 804 WebArena tasks, 45 non-visual MiniWoB++ tasks over 50 seeds, five CRM tasks over 20 scenarios, and a smaller live-site study. On WebArena, SteP raises success from the cited 14.9% prior result to 33.5% and beats a flat 8k-context policy while using about 2.3 times less context. Ablations compare flat prompts, static hierarchy, policy-history sharing, and policy-library composition.

## Limitations and critical assessment

Subtasks and prompts are manually authored using domain knowledge, so the method shifts part of agent design into policy-library engineering. Incorrect handoffs, insufficient arguments, or missing information can cause loops, and stack-local context may hide globally relevant state. More calls add latency, while reported context savings are not a full cost comparison. Results rely heavily on GPT-4-era policies and benchmark environments; the small live-site evaluation cannot establish robustness to changing interfaces. Dynamic composition improves modularity but does not itself provide safety constraints on which policy may act.

## Relevance to the survey

SteP is direct evidence for hierarchical, modular web-agent control and context isolation. It shows that reusable subpolicies can outperform a single long prompt, while its limitations motivate explicit handoff contracts, shared belief-state checks, loop detection, and per-policy permissions.
