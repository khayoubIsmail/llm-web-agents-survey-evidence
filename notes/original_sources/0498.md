## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

MOBA targets mobile GUIs whose action spaces and visual structure change across pages. It combines a global planning agent, a local execution agent, reflection-based recovery, and multifaceted memory. The paper also introduces MobBench to score complete and partial progress on complex mobile tasks.

### Method and evaluation

The global agent decomposes a request into adaptable subgoals and checks their feasibility; the local agent uses current GUI observations and retrieved experience to act. Reflection detects failed or infeasible subgoals, while memory stores user, task, and interaction information. MobBench and AndroidArena evaluations compare general multimodal agents and mobile-specific baselines. The paper reports improvements over GPT-4 of 2.4 and 14.3 points on two highlighted task measures/settings. Ablations show that planning and memory are complementary, with planning particularly helpful for long, cross-application tasks.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Complete-task rates remain low for all agents, which is why milestone scores are necessary. Task completion judgment itself can be unreliable, visual models miss UI details, and view-hierarchy text is sometimes required. The architecture adds cloud calls and stores sensitive interaction history, creating privacy and latency concerns not resolved by benchmark gains.

### Relevance to the survey

MOBA supports two-level planning and partial-progress evaluation in mobile/web interfaces. It also shows why GUI agents need both pixels and structured accessibility information.
