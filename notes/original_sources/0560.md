## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

AutoManual lets agents build an explicit instruction manual through interaction. A Planner executes from current rules, a Builder adds, edits, or consolidates rules from feedback using a structured rule system, and a Formulator produces a human-readable manual. Case-conditioned prompting is used to reduce hallucinated rule updates.

### Method and evaluation

Starting from one demonstration, rules are optimized online and tested on ALFWorld, MiniWoB++, and WebArena-related settings. Reported success reaches 97.4% with GPT-4-Turbo and 86.2% with GPT-3.5-Turbo on ALFWorld, and 98.3%/92.7% on MiniWoB++. Ablations show that removing rule types or case conditioning reduces success—for example, one hallucination-control comparison rises from 93.8% to 97.4%. Manuals can also guide smaller models, though gains are smaller in some transfer settings.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Online rules are path dependent: early mistakes can bias later exploration. High results come from benchmarks with reusable regularities and feedback; live sites change and may present conflicting cases. A human-readable manual is inspectable but not automatically correct, and multiple LLM calls add cost.

### Relevance to the survey

AutoManual supports persistent procedural memory for web agents. Rules should remain linked to source trajectories and be revalidated against current interfaces.
