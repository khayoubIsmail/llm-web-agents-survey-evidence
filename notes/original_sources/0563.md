# Re-ReST: Reflection-Reinforced Self-Training for Language Agents

## Research problem and contribution

Re-ReST improves self-training when an agent's own samples are too poor to learn from. A reflector receives a failed output plus environment feedback—such as unit-test errors—and rewrites it into a higher-quality training example.

## Method and evaluation

The study spans multi-hop QA, sequential decision-making, code generation, visual QA, and text-to-image generation with open-source agents. Plain self-training improves HotpotQA by 7.6% and ALFWorld by 28.4% over the stated baselines; adding reflection contributes a further 2.0% and 14.1%, respectively. On one ALFWorld comparison, success rises from 8.9% to 37.3%, with the reflector accounting for a 14.1-point uplift. Ablations compare selection, reflection, and inference-time use.

## Limitations and critical assessment

Training assumes ground-truth environmental feedback. Where correctness is subjective or only apparent after long execution, the reflector lacks a reliable anchor. Rewriting failures can also produce plausible but unexecuted pseudo-successes unless every revision is rechecked. The method reduces dependence on a stronger teacher, not dependence on evaluators.

## Relevance to the survey

Use for verified self-training in web agents. A failed trajectory should only enter training after the reflected revision is executed and its outcome recorded.
