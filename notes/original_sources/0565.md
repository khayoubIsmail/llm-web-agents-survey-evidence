# Auto-Intent: Automated Intent Discovery and Self-Exploration for Large Language Model Web Agents

## Research problem and contribution

Auto-Intent adapts a frozen LLM to web navigation by discovering compact latent intents from demonstrations. An intent predictor maps interaction history to several likely next intents, and the policy treats the top-k phrases as alternative semantic directions for action.

## Method and evaluation

An LLM extracts unsupervised intent labels of at most three words from Mind2Web demonstrations. A smaller predictor is trained on those labels; at inference, top-k diverse intents support “self-exploration” before the frozen policy selects among candidate elements. Tests use Mind2Web cross-task, cross-website, and cross-domain splits with GPT-3.5/4 and Llama-3.1-70B/405B, plus transfer to WebArena shopping. Auto-Intent improves every tested backbone in the main table; in the displayed WebArena transfer, GPT-4 task success rises from 19.0% to 23.8% with the learned intent component.

## Limitations and critical assessment

Evaluation is limited to web navigation, and intent quality depends on unsupervised labels that may merge distinct actions or leak demonstrator conventions. Top-k hints add diversity but can distract the policy. Cross-benchmark transfer is promising but restricted to a subset/domain and does not establish general domain adaptation.

## Relevance to the survey

Auto-Intent supports a compact intermediate action abstraction between raw DOM elements and low-level clicks. Such intents can improve interpretability if linked to the actual observation and action chosen.
