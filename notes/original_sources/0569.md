### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

Learn-by-interact generates agent training data from environment documentation and executable interaction, using backward construction and filtering rather than manually labeled trajectories. In the paper's main comparisons, Claude on OSWorld improves from 12.4% to 22.5% with retrieved interaction knowledge, while Codestral-22B on WebArena improves from 4.7% to 24.2% after training on synthesized interaction data. The experiments show that environment-derived supervision can substantially improve both in-context and fine-tuned agents without hand-authoring every trajectory.

#### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

The quality of synthesized tasks is bounded by documentation coverage and the backward-construction/filtering pipeline. Environments with sparse documentation or hidden state can yield incomplete supervision, and generated interactions may not reflect real user distributions.

### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** Synthetic trajectories can contain artifacts.
- **Limitation 2:** Data quality matters more than raw volume.
- **Limitation 3:** Training data may not generalize to changing websites or new apps.

General thesis-level limitation:

```text
Training improvements in one environment do not automatically imply generalized web automation.
Agent learning still depends on perception, action grounding, evaluation validity, safety, and environment realism.
```

Therefore, use this paper as **P2 support** unless it becomes central to the S5.4 subsection.
