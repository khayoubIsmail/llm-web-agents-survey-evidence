# R-Judge: Benchmarking Safety Risk Awareness for LLM Agents

## Research problem and contribution

R-Judge evaluates whether an LLM can act as a safety monitor over a completed agent trajectory rather than merely classify the harmlessness of isolated generated text. Its 569 human-annotated, multi-turn interaction records span 27 scenarios, five application categories, and ten risk types; each record includes the user instruction, agent actions, environment feedback, a binary safety label, and a risk description.

## Method and evaluation

The authors curate intended-attack and unintended-risk cases from agent execution records and evaluate 11 API and open models on two linked tasks: binary safety judgment and open-ended risk identification. The standard pipeline asks a model to analyze a record and then label it safe or unsafe. GPT-4o is the strongest general model but reaches only 74.45 F1 on safety judgment; other models generally remain much weaker. Adding risk-type hints or two-shot demonstrations does not consistently improve results. A safety-specific Meta-Llama-Guard-2-8B reaches 71.84 F1, supporting the paper's finding that targeted safety fine-tuning transfers better than simple prompt changes. Manual error analysis attributes failures to weak scenario simulation, condition-dependent risk understanding, and divergence from human judgments.

## Limitations and critical assessment

R-Judge is a monitor benchmark, not an intervention or prevention study: it tests recognition from logged trajectories after actions have been proposed or executed. Its 569 records are relatively small, selected from a bounded set of applications, and the binary label compresses severity, reversibility, uncertainty, and responsibility. The open-ended risk score relies on GPT-4 pairwise evaluation, which introduces evaluator dependence. The study treats the acting agent as benign but fallible, so its results should not be generalized to adaptive malicious agents. Stronger performance after safety fine-tuning also does not establish robustness to distribution shift or deliberate evasion.

## Relevance to the survey

Use R-Judge as evidence that behavioral risk awareness requires reasoning over action history and environmental consequences. It supports a distinction between content moderation and trajectory-level agent monitoring, while showing that prompt-only safeguards are not a dependable substitute for specialized data and evaluation.
