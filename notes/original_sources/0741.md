# The Emerged Security and Privacy of LLM Agent: A Survey with Case Studies

## Research problem and contribution

This survey organizes security and privacy risks for LLM agents around inherited LLM vulnerabilities and agent-specific threats arising from instructions, tools, knowledge, memory, and environmental interaction. It maps attacks, impacts, and defenses and uses a virtual-town setting to illustrate how risks affect users, environments, and other agents. It separately discusses multimodal agents and multi-agent systems as emerging risk amplifiers.

## Method and evidence base

The paper is a narrative taxonomy and case-study synthesis rather than a systematic review with a reproducible search protocol. It describes technical vulnerabilities such as hallucination, ambiguity, catastrophic forgetting, and bias; malicious attacks including jailbreaking, knowledge poisoning, output manipulation, and functional manipulation; and corresponding defensive techniques. Summary tables compare mechanisms, reported performance, and limitations. The virtual-town examples show how inherited model weaknesses can combine with action and communication channels. Future-trend sections emphasize multimodal attack surfaces and cascading misinformation or malicious behavior in multi-agent systems.

## Limitations and critical assessment

The paper does not report databases searched, inclusion dates, screening criteria, duplicate handling, or a formal quality assessment, so coverage and selection bias cannot be audited. Many performance figures are transcribed from heterogeneous source studies whose tasks and metrics are not directly comparable. The case studies are explanatory illustrations rather than controlled evaluations of the proposed taxonomy. Several recommendations—such as decentralized security or differential privacy for multi-agent exchange—are prospective and not validated within the paper. Bibliographic and template artifacts in the manuscript also warrant caution when using venue or publication metadata.

## Relevance to the survey

Use this work as a secondary source for broad threat categories and for the claim that agent components create new paths for inherited LLM weaknesses to produce external consequences. Primary studies should support quantitative claims. Its multi-agent discussion is particularly helpful for highlighting propagation and trust-boundary risks beyond a single web-agent loop.
