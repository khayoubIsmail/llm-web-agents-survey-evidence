## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

This survey proposes a risk taxonomy tied to six features of LLM-based agents and covers security, privacy, and ethics threats across problematic inputs, model flaws, and input-model interaction. It synthesizes attack and defense work, then analyzes WebGPT, Voyager, PReP, and ChatDev as case studies to show how web access, embodiment, multimodality, and multi-agent collaboration change threat severity.

### Method and evidence base

The paper is a broad narrative survey. Threat sections cover adversarial inputs, goal hijacking, model extraction, prompt leakage, jailbreaks, hallucination, memorization, bias, backdoors, and data leakage, with discussion of both attacks and defenses. The four agent case studies map these categories onto distinct architectures: search and browsing, embodied exploration, multimodal navigation, and collaborative software development. From those mappings the authors propose research needs in data support, theoretical and interpretability-driven methods, agent-specific defenses, and governance.

### Limitations and critical assessment

No reproducible literature-search or study-selection procedure is provided, and the covered fields evolve quickly, so completeness cannot be assumed. The taxonomy mixes attacks, intrinsic errors, privacy leakage, and ethical harms at different abstraction levels. Reported results come from heterogeneous benchmarks and are not meta-analyzed. The four case studies are conceptual applications of the taxonomy, not attacks reproduced against the systems, so statements about increased severity are plausible mechanisms rather than measured comparative effects. Several future-policy recommendations are normative and lack implementation or evaluation evidence.

### Relevance to the survey

This is useful as a cross-check on threat coverage and as a structured reminder that web agents add multiple input sources, repeated interactions, specialized domains, and external actions. It should guide synthesis and gap identification, while primary empirical papers remain the evidentiary basis for numerical or causal claims.
