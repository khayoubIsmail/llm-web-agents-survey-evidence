### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

The paper demonstrates that tool selection is strongly affected by natural-language tool descriptions even when functionality is unchanged. Carefully edited descriptions cause a target tool to receive more than ten times the usage of the original description for GPT-4.1 and Qwen2.5-7B. Experiments across 17 models show that the exact preference patterns vary by model but description-induced bias is widespread, creating both reliability and marketplace/security concerns for MCP/function-calling agents.

#### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

The experiments isolate tool-description competition rather than complete malicious tool execution, and preferences can shift with model/version changes. It demonstrates an unstable selection interface, not universal exploitability.
