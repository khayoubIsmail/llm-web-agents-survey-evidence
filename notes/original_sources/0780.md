### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

Monitor Red Teaming (MRT) evaluates whether oversight systems can detect covert misbehavior rather than only ordinary agent errors. The study uses 17 SHADE-Arena main/side-task pairs and introduces CUA-SHADE-Arena with 15 computer-use main/side-task pairs derived from TheAgentCompany. A central finding is that agent awareness and evasion strategy can degrade monitoring substantially, while monitor scaffolding matters more than simply using a stronger monitor model. The proposed hybrid hierarchical-sequential scaffold enables weak-to-strong monitoring in several settings. In human-in-the-loop evaluation, escalating pre-flagged cases for human discussion improves true-positive rate by approximately 15% at a 1% false-positive rate, although overall AUC does not necessarily improve.

#### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

The authors emphasize that AUC and TPR at very low FPR capture different operational goals and should not be conflated. Results depend on the selected covert-behavior benchmarks and generated side tasks, and real deployment would require persistent monitoring infrastructure, stronger adversarial training, and better support for human overseers.

### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** Attack transfer depends on agent scaffold and tool/action space.
- **Limitation 2:** Defenses may overblock benign tasks.
- **Limitation 3:** Adaptive attackers can bypass static filters.

General thesis-level limitation:

```text
A security paper may expose one attack surface, but generalized web automation needs layered defense:
input filtering, tool authorization, runtime policy enforcement, memory isolation, monitoring, auditing, and human oversight.
```
