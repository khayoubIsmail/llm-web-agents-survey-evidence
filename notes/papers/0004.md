### Paper 4 — Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models

#### Paper contribution

Wang et al. propose **self-consistency**, a decoding strategy for chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. Instead of taking a single greedy reasoning trajectory, the model samples multiple diverse reasoning paths and selects the answer receiving the largest aggregated support. The method changes inference/decoding rather than model parameters.

#### Method

1. Prompt the model with chain-of-thought exemplars.
2. Sample multiple reasoning paths using stochastic decoding.
3. Extract the final answer from each path.
4. Marginalize/vote over the sampled paths and return the most consistent answer.

The rationale is that difficult reasoning problems can admit many valid solution paths even when the final answer is unique. Agreement across independently sampled paths can therefore suppress idiosyncratic reasoning errors.

#### Experimental scope

The paper evaluates four large language-model families/scales across arithmetic and commonsense reasoning tasks. Reported benchmarks include GSM8K, SVAMP, AQuA, MultiArith, AddSub, SingleEq, CommonsenseQA, StrategyQA, and ARC-Challenge, with additional robustness and calibration analyses in the appendices.

#### Concrete results

Representative absolute gains over ordinary CoT decoding include:

- **GSM8K:** +17.9 percentage points.
- **SVAMP:** +11.0 points.
- **AQuA:** +12.2 points.
- **StrategyQA:** +6.4 points.
- **ARC-Challenge:** +3.9 points.

For PaLM-540B on the arithmetic suite, Table 2 reports CoT→self-consistency changes including **GSM8K 56.5→74.4**, **SVAMP 79.0→86.6**, and **AQuA 35.8→48.3**. With code-davinci-002, GSM8K improves **60.1→78.0** and SVAMP **75.8→86.8**.

#### Robustness and uncertainty evidence

- The method remains beneficial across different prompt sets. On PaLM-540B GSM8K, three prompt sets show CoT accuracies 56.5/54.6/54.0 versus self-consistency 74.4/72.1/70.4.
- Accuracy generally saturates as more paths are sampled; the authors suggest that **5–10 paths** can recover much of the gain when compute is constrained.
- Agreement among sampled answers can also provide uncertainty/calibration information, so the technique is relevant not only as a performance method but also as a confidence signal.

#### Limitations

- **Inference cost increases directly with the number of sampled reasoning paths.** This is the principal limitation stated in the conclusion.
- Majority agreement is not a correctness proof: correlated model errors can produce a confidently wrong consensus.
- The method assumes that final answers can be extracted and aggregated; open-ended action sequences or structured web trajectories do not always admit a simple voting operation.
- The experiments concern reasoning benchmarks rather than browser interaction, tool execution, provenance verification, or stateful environment control.
- Self-consistency improves inference without addressing whether the generated rationales are faithful explanations of the model's internal computation.

#### Relevance to the survey

Self-consistency is a P0 reasoning/verification primitive for agents. An agent can sample alternative plans, extraction interpretations, or candidate outputs and use agreement as one signal before execution. However, for web automation it must be combined with environment-grounded verification, because agreement among language-model trajectories alone cannot establish that a DOM action, retrieved fact, or extracted field is correct.

#### Synthesis statement

Self-consistency replaces single-path greedy CoT decoding with sampled-path marginalization and produces large reasoning gains such as +17.9 points on GSM8K; it is therefore useful as an agent-level deliberation/consensus primitive, but it multiplies inference cost and cannot substitute for grounding or external-state verification.
