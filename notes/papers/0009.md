### Paper 9 — Finetuned Language Models Are Zero-Shot Learners (FLAN)

#### Paper contribution

Wei et al. introduce **instruction tuning** as a way to improve zero-shot generalization. A pretrained language model is fine-tuned on a mixture of tasks expressed through natural-language instructions and then evaluated on **held-out task types**, so the evaluation measures transfer to task families not used during instruction tuning rather than memorization of the same supervised task.

#### Method

- Base model: **LaMDA-PT, 137B parameters**, dense decoder-only Transformer.
- Pretraining corpus: web documents, dialogue data, Wikipedia, and code; the paper reports roughly **2.49T BPE tokens**, with about 10% non-English data.
- Instruction-tuning mixture: **more than 60 NLP datasets**, converted into natural-language instructional templates.
- Tasks are grouped into clusters. For each evaluation cluster, that task type is held out while the model is instruction-tuned on the remaining clusters.
- Evaluation spans NLI, reading comprehension, closed-book QA, commonsense reasoning, sentiment, paraphrase, translation, structured-to-text generation, and other task families.

#### Concrete results

- Zero-shot FLAN outperforms zero-shot **GPT-3 175B on 20 of 25 evaluated datasets**.
- Using the best development template, FLAN also exceeds GPT-3 few-shot performance on **10 datasets**.
- The paper highlights large improvements over few-shot GPT-3 on ANLI, RTE, BoolQ, AI2-ARC, OpenBookQA, and StoryCloze.
- Against GLaM 64B/64E, best-template zero-shot FLAN outperforms zero-shot GLaM on **13 of 19** available datasets and one-shot GLaM on **11 of 19**.

#### Ablation evidence

Three ablations are central to the claim:

1. **Task diversity:** increasing the number of instruction-tuning clusters improves held-out-task performance, with no saturation observed over the tested one-to-seven-cluster range.
2. **Model scale:** instruction-tuning benefits are weak/negative at small scales and emerge strongly at larger sizes; the study evaluates 422M, 2B, 8B, 68B, and 137B models.
3. **Natural-language instructions:** removing instructions from the fine-tuning formulation hurts transfer, showing that success is not merely multi-task supervised training without task descriptions.

The paper also reports a negative result: instruction tuning is not useful when the downstream task essentially matches the base language-modeling objective and instructions are redundant.

#### Limitations and failure cases

- Task-cluster assignment has subjective judgment; alternative grouping could change what counts as held-out transfer.
- Instructions are relatively short, typically one sentence, so the study does not establish behavior under long or complex operational policies.
- Pretraining-data overlap with benchmark examples cannot be completely ruled out; the authors perform a post-hoc contamination analysis and do not find evidence that overlap explains the main gains.
- FLAN's context length is only **1024 tokens**, limiting long-document tasks such as summarization.
- The model is mostly trained on English data and does not establish broad multilingual robustness.
- FLAN fails some very simple tasks in qualitative examples, including returning a requested word position and following a language-specific answer instruction.
- A 137B model is costly to serve.

#### Relevance to LLM agents

FLAN is a P1 foundation for the natural-language **instruction interface** used by agents. It provides evidence that multi-task instruction tuning can make unseen task descriptions executable without task-specific fine-tuning. However, the paper's tasks are primarily text transformations/QA rather than interactive stateful environments, so it does not demonstrate browser grounding, action verification, long-horizon recovery, or schema-valid extraction.

#### Synthesis statement

FLAN shows that instruction tuning a 137B model on more than 60 instructed datasets can substantially improve unseen-task zero-shot transfer—beating zero-shot GPT-3 on 20/25 datasets—but its gains depend on scale and task diversity, and its short-context, English-heavy, non-interactive evaluation leaves agent grounding and reliable action execution unresolved.
