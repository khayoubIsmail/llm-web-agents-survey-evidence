### Paper-specific synthesis

#### Contribution and method

Dosovitskiy et al. show that image recognition can be performed by a nearly standard Transformer without a convolutional backbone. An image is split into fixed-size patches, each patch is linearly embedded, positional embeddings and a learnable classification token are added, and the resulting token sequence is processed by a Transformer encoder. ViT therefore moves the token-sequence abstraction from language to vision.

The main variants are ViT-Base (12 layers, 86M parameters), ViT-Large (24 layers, 307M), and ViT-Huge (32 layers, 632M). The authors pretrain on ImageNet (1.3M images), ImageNet-21k (14M), and JFT-300M (303M), then transfer to ImageNet/ReaL, CIFAR-10/100, Oxford Pets/Flowers and the 19-task VTAB suite.

#### Concrete findings

- ViT-H/14 pretrained on JFT-300M reaches **88.55% ImageNet**, **90.72% ImageNet-ReaL**, **99.50% CIFAR-10**, **94.55% CIFAR-100**, **97.56% Oxford Pets**, and **77.63% VTAB**.
- Its pretraining cost is reported as about **2.5k TPUv3-core-days**, compared with **9.9k** for BiT-L and **12.3k** for Noisy Student in the paper's comparison.
- ViT-L/16 pretrained on public ImageNet-21k obtains **85.30% ImageNet** and **72.72% VTAB** with about **0.23k TPUv3-core-days**.
- The authors find that ViT is weaker than comparably sized ResNets when trained only on moderate-size data, but the gap reverses as pretraining data grows. They attribute this partly to ViT's weaker built-in locality/translation inductive bias.

#### Relevance to Web agents

ViT is enabling evidence for screenshot-based Web agents: it establishes that visual interfaces can be represented as sequences of patch tokens and processed with Transformer attention. This architectural line underlies later multimodal LLMs used for GUI and browser perception. It does not itself evaluate browser actions, element grounding, OCR, task planning, or extraction.

#### Limitations

The strongest results depend on very large supervised pretraining corpora, including proprietary JFT-300M. ViT has less image-specific inductive bias than CNNs and performs worse when data is insufficient. The study is image classification/transfer rather than interactive perception; therefore high recognition accuracy cannot be treated as evidence of reliable GUI grounding.

#### Evidence locations

Method: Section 3. Model/data setup: Section 4.1 and Table 1. State-of-the-art comparison: Section 4.2 and Table 2. Data-requirement/scaling analyses: Sections 4.3–4.4. Attention/representation analyses: Section 4.5 and appendices.

#### One-sentence synthesis

ViT demonstrates that a standard Transformer over image patches can outperform strong CNN transfer baselines when pretrained at sufficient scale, providing an architectural foundation for screenshot encoders while leaving interactive Web grounding and action selection unevaluated.
