### Paper-specific synthesis

#### Research question

Sprague et al. test a broad assumption in LLM evaluation: that asking a model to produce chain-of-thought should generally improve reasoning. They first synthesize more than **100 prior papers**, then run a controlled comparison over **20 datasets and 14 models**, separating mathematical/symbolic tasks from commonsense, knowledge and other forms of reasoning.

#### Main finding

The benefit of chain-of-thought is highly task dependent. Strong gains are concentrated in mathematics and explicitly symbolic problems, while many knowledge and commonsense datasets show little improvement, no improvement or degradation relative to direct answering. This means that “reasoning” benchmarks should not automatically be equated with a need for visible chain-of-thought.

#### Concrete evidence

The paper's MMLU analysis separates questions according to whether the question or model response contains an equals sign, used as a simple marker for mathematical/symbolic content. For Llama-3.1-70B on MMLU, the total CoT gain is only about **1.9 points**, with roughly **94% of that gain** attributable to the equation-marked slice. On MMLU-Pro, Llama-3.1-70B gains **11.4 points**, of which **11.1 points / 97.6%** are associated with that slice. Similar concentration appears for several other models.

Across non-mathematical benchmarks, the full result table contains many cases where direct answering is equal or better; for example GPT-4o on PiQA is **95.9 direct vs 95.5 with CoT**, and Llama-3.1-70B on ARC-Challenge is **95.0 vs 93.6**. The authors also show that dedicated symbolic tools/solvers can sometimes provide the relevant benefit more efficiently than unrestricted natural-language CoT.

#### Relevance to agents

Web agents often incur substantial token/latency cost by generating reasoning before every action. This paper provides evidence against a universal “always reason verbosely” policy. An adaptive agent should reserve expensive reasoning for states that benefit from decomposition or symbolic manipulation and use direct actions for simpler retrieval/grounding steps when sufficient.

The study does not evaluate browser trajectories, however; its implication is about reasoning-policy design rather than proof that shorter Web-agent trajectories are more successful.

#### Limitations

The reasoning categories and equation-marker analysis are useful diagnostics but do not perfectly capture all forms of reasoning. Results are model/prompt/dataset dependent, and newer models may internalize different amounts of reasoning. Accuracy comparisons also do not measure faithfulness of generated reasoning.

#### Evidence locations

Experimental design and task taxonomy: early method sections. Cross-dataset direct-vs-CoT results: main tables/figures. MMLU/MMLU-Pro mathematical-slice analysis: Section on why MMLU gains, Appendix G and Tables 18–19. Conclusions and deployment implications: Section 7.

#### One-sentence synthesis

Across 20 datasets and 14 models, chain-of-thought benefits are concentrated in mathematical and symbolic reasoning rather than being universally useful, supporting adaptive reasoning policies for agents instead of unconditional verbose deliberation.
