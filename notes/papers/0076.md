### Paper 76 — A Survey on Large Language Model based Autonomous Agents

#### Paper contribution

Wang et al. provide an early systematic survey of LLM-based autonomous agents, organizing the field around **construction, applications, and evaluation**. The paper states that it compiled **100 relevant agent works** and proposes a unified architectural view rather than evaluating one new agent. Its value for this survey is therefore secondary-source taxonomy and terminology, not primary benchmark evidence.

#### Unified agent-construction framework

The architecture taxonomy contains four modules:

1. **Profiling:** define the role/persona/task identity that conditions behavior.
2. **Memory:** store and retrieve observations and experience.
3. **Planning:** formulate future actions, with or without feedback.
4. **Action:** translate decisions into interactions/outputs.

The paper emphasizes dependencies among these modules: profiling conditions memory/planning; memory provides accumulated experience; planning selects future behavior; and action operationalizes decisions in the environment.

#### Profiling taxonomy

Three profile-construction strategies are distinguished:

- **Handcrafted profiles** — flexible and controllable but labor-intensive at scale.
- **LLM-generated profiles** — scalable but less controllable.
- **Dataset-aligned profiles** — grounded in real populations/data but limited by available data and representation choices.

This section matters mainly for social/simulated agents; it is less central to web automation, where “profile” is better interpreted as task role, permissions, and policy context rather than human-like persona.

#### Memory taxonomy

The survey distinguishes memory by structure, representation, and operation:

- short-term/context-window vs external long-term memory;
- natural-language vs embedding-based memory;
- operations of **reading, writing, and reflection**.

For reading, the survey highlights recency, relevance, and importance as common retrieval criteria. For writing, it discusses duplicate information and overflow/forgetting; examples include consolidation after N=5 related action sequences and FIFO-style fixed buffers. Reflection summarizes lower-level experiences into higher-level insights.

#### Planning taxonomy

The survey separates:

- **planning without feedback**, where a plan is generated before execution;
- **planning with feedback**, where environmental outcomes or internal evaluation revise the plan.

The authors stress that long-horizon tasks make fixed initial plans brittle because preconditions and environment state are difficult to predict perfectly. Feedback therefore becomes a central capability for autonomous behavior.

#### Action and capability acquisition

The action module encompasses textual outputs, tool/API use, and embodied/digital environment actions. Beyond architecture, the paper categorizes how agents acquire capability depending on whether the underlying LLM is kept frozen and prompted/scaffolded or further trained/fine-tuned. This distinction is useful for separating **agent scaffolding** from **model adaptation**.

#### Applications and evaluation

The survey groups applications across social simulation, natural-science/engineering tasks, and other domains, then reviews evaluation strategies including subjective human evaluation and objective benchmark/task metrics. The evaluation discussion is broad; it should not substitute for primary benchmark papers when reporting numerical agent performance.

#### Challenges identified by the authors

The final section organizes major challenges including:

- role-playing fidelity;
- long-term planning and robustness;
- generalized human alignment;
- prompt injection/adversarial robustness and trustworthiness;
- evaluation standardization;
- **efficiency**, because an autonomous agent may query an autoregressive LLM multiple times for memory retrieval, planning, and each action.

The paper notes that deploying multiple agents compounds latency/cost and that LLM inference speed directly constrains agent action efficiency.

#### Limitations and evidentiary boundary

- This is a **secondary survey**, so claims about specific agent effectiveness should be traced to primary studies.
- The field snapshot is rapidly time-sensitive; taxonomies based on the early 2023–2024 agent ecosystem can become incomplete as tool protocols, multimodal computer-use agents, and evaluation benchmarks evolve.
- The four-module architecture is useful but coarse: provenance, validation, permissions, error recovery, orchestration, and data-quality processes are not first-class modules.
- The review covers 100 selected works but does not serve as a formal risk-of-bias or reproducibility audit of each primary study.

#### Relevance to generalized web automation and extraction

This paper provides P1 background for describing an agent as more than an LLM: memory and planning mediate between a task profile and environment actions. For generalized web/data agents, its architecture should be extended with explicit **observation/grounding, provenance, schema validation, safety/permission checks, and recovery**, which are central to extraction quality but not emphasized in the four-module framework.

#### Synthesis statement

This survey systematizes roughly 100 early LLM-agent works through profiling, memory, planning, and action modules and highlights feedback, evaluation and efficiency as unresolved issues. It is useful architectural background, but primary studies should support performance claims and a web-extraction framework needs additional first-class components for grounding, provenance, validation, permissions, and recovery.
