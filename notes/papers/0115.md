### Paper-specific synthesis

#### Scope and organizing questions

Guo et al. survey LLM-based multi-agent systems around three concrete questions: **what domains/environments multi-agent systems operate in; how individual agents are profiled and communicate; and how the agents acquire or improve capabilities**. The IJCAI Survey Track version is a compact synthesis intended to organize a rapidly growing literature rather than introduce a new benchmark.

#### Architectural synthesis

The paper distinguishes systems by the environments they simulate or solve tasks within, by the construction of agent identities/roles, and by their communication/interaction structures. Profiling can be manually specified or generated, while communication can be cooperative, competitive, debate-like, hierarchical or otherwise structured by the task. The survey also discusses mechanisms that improve collective capability, including feedback, memory/experience, reflection, role specialization and coordination protocols.

A key point for evidence use is that multi-agent gains cannot be attributed simply to the number of agents: outcomes depend on **role design, communication quality, information sharing, coordination and the reliability of the underlying LLMs**. The paper also inventories commonly used datasets/benchmarks and maintains a public literature repository.

#### Relevance to generalized Web agents

The survey supports architectural discussion of Web systems that divide work among a navigator, extractor, verifier or planner. It is useful for explaining role specialization and communication topology, especially when several agents jointly search sources or verify extracted records.

It is secondary evidence, however. It does not establish that multi-agent systems outperform single agents on generalized Web automation, and it should not replace primary benchmark papers when quantitative claims are made.

#### Challenges and limitations

The survey highlights coordination overhead, communication inefficiency, hallucination/error propagation, evaluation difficulty, safety/alignment concerns and the lack of standardized benchmarks. Because the area changes rapidly, its taxonomy is necessarily time-bounded. Many reviewed systems use bespoke environments, making cross-system comparison difficult.

#### One-sentence synthesis

This IJCAI survey organizes LLM multi-agent systems by environment, profiling, communication and capability development, supporting structured multi-agent Web-agent taxonomy while emphasizing that coordination and evaluation remain unresolved bottlenecks.
