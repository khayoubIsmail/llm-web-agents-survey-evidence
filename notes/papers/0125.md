### Paper-specific synthesis

#### Method

Graph of Thoughts (GoT) generalizes linear Chain-of-Thought and tree-based search by representing reasoning as an **arbitrary directed graph of intermediate thoughts**. Operations can generate new thoughts, score them, aggregate several partial solutions, or refine an existing thought. This permits branches to merge and previously generated information to be recombined rather than forcing reasoning into a chain or tree.

#### Concrete evidence

The paper evaluates GoT on algorithmic and aggregation-heavy tasks such as sorting, set intersection and document merging. On sorting, the authors report that GoT improves solution quality by **more than 62% relative to Tree of Thoughts** while reducing the associated computational/token cost by **more than 31%** in the reported configuration. The advantage is strongest on tasks where partial results can be solved independently and then merged, because graph aggregation explicitly represents that structure.

#### Relevance to Web agents

Many Web tasks are naturally graph-structured rather than purely sequential: several pages can be explored independently, candidate records can be validated in parallel, and partial evidence can later be merged. GoT therefore provides a useful reasoning abstraction for multi-source extraction and coverage planning, especially where independent subproblems converge into one final dataset.

#### Limitations

The graph topology and operations are designed for each task; GoT is therefore not a fully automatic planner. More graph branches can increase LLM calls, and scoring/aggregation remain model-dependent. The evaluated problems are controlled reasoning tasks rather than interactive websites, so graph reasoning does not by itself solve grounding, page-state tracking or safe action execution.

#### One-sentence synthesis

Graph of Thoughts shows that allowing reasoning branches to split, merge and be refined can outperform tree-structured reasoning on compositional tasks while reducing cost, providing a useful abstraction for multi-source agent planning rather than direct Web-interaction evidence.
