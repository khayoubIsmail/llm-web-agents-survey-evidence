### Paper-specific synthesis

#### Scope

PlanGenLLMs surveys LLM planning as the transformation of an initial world state into a sequence of actions intended to reach a goal state. It explicitly connects classical planning criteria to modern LLM planners and covers diverse domains including **Web navigation, travel planning, embodied tasks, games and database/query planning**.

#### Six evaluation criteria

The paper's main contribution is a six-dimensional framework for evaluating generated plans:

1. **Completeness** — whether the plan covers all steps needed for the goal.
2. **Executability** — whether actions can actually be carried out under environment constraints.
3. **Optimality** — whether the plan avoids unnecessary cost/steps relative to alternatives.
4. **Representation** — how plans and environment/action information are encoded.
5. **Generalization** — whether planning transfers to unseen tasks, environments or problem scales.
6. **Efficiency** — computational, interaction and token/action cost.

This framework exposes why simple final success rate is insufficient: two agents can reach the same goal with very different validity, cost and robustness.

#### Findings relevant to agents

The survey shows that LLM planners vary widely in whether they generate plans directly, use external planners/tools, search over candidate plans, incorporate feedback, or ground planning in environment state. It emphasizes persistent gaps in reliable executability and generalization: fluent plans can violate action preconditions or environmental constraints, while task-specific systems can appear strong without transferring to new domains.

#### Relevance to generalized Web automation

The six criteria provide a directly useful lens for Web-agent planning. A browser trajectory should not only finish a task; its actions must be executable on the observed page, cover required subtasks, avoid excessive exploration, use a representation that preserves relevant state, transfer across websites, and remain efficient. This helps separate planning quality from grounding/execution quality in the survey taxonomy.

#### Limitations

PlanGenLLMs is a secondary synthesis, not a controlled benchmark. The compared studies use different models, tasks, environments and metrics, so the survey cannot support a universal quantitative ranking of planning methods. Rapid model changes can also date benchmark conclusions.

#### Evidence locations

Survey motivation/domain scope: introduction. Six performance criteria and representative methods: core taxonomy sections. Web-navigation examples and task domains: application comparisons. Open challenges and evaluation inconsistency: discussion/future-directions section.

#### One-sentence synthesis

PlanGenLLMs frames LLM planning quality through completeness, executability, optimality, representation, generalization and efficiency, providing a strong evaluation taxonomy for Web-agent planning while remaining secondary rather than controlled empirical evidence.
