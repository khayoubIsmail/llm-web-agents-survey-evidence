## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

This survey organizes methods that automatically correct LLM outputs using feedback generated by the model itself or by another automated component. Its central contribution is a process-oriented taxonomy separating training-time correction, generation-time correction, and post-hoc correction. It also distinguishes feedback sources, verification mechanisms, and the stage at which a correction changes model behavior.

### Method and evidence base

The paper is a structured literature review rather than a new benchmark. It compares self-correction, external critics, verifier-guided decoding, iterative refinement, retrieval or tool-supported checking, and training procedures that learn from automatically detected errors. The organization is useful because superficially similar “reflection” systems may operate at very different points in the pipeline and make different assumptions about access to ground truth.

### Findings and critical assessment

Automated correction can improve factuality, reasoning, constraint following, and safety, but the survey does not support a blanket claim that self-correction is reliable. Correction quality is bounded by the feedback source: a model that cannot recognize an error may reinforce it, while external tools or verifiers add coverage and integration constraints. Iteration also increases cost and can degrade a correct first answer. Because the reviewed studies use heterogeneous tasks and metrics, the taxonomy is stronger evidence than any cross-paper performance comparison.

### Relevance to the survey

Use this paper to distinguish reflection from verified correction in web agents. A web workflow should specify who or what produces feedback, what evidence it sees, and whether correction occurs during planning, execution, or after extraction.
