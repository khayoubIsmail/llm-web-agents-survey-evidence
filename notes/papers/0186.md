## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

CRAFT addresses a limitation of large, fixed API collections: general-purpose tools may not match the operations needed for a new task. It contributes a plug-and-play pipeline that creates a task-specific toolset from training examples, validates and abstracts successful code solutions, deduplicates them, and retrieves relevant tools at inference time without fine-tuning the target LLM.

### Method and evaluation

GPT-4 first produces executable solutions for training examples. Correct solutions are converted into reusable functions, filtered, and indexed using multiple views such as the problem, function name, and documentation. At inference, the model retrieves and either executes or conditions on selected functions. Evaluation covers GQA, OK-VQA, and A-OKVQA for visual reasoning, TabMWP for table processing, and the algebra subset of MATH. The paper reports an average 43.16% relative F1 improvement over the strongest baselines in the stated comparison, plus sizeable soft-accuracy gains over ViperGPT. Ablations attribute gains to both abstraction and retrieval, while scale analyses show benefits from more tools and stronger backbones.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Tool creation depends on a powerful model and executable training examples, so cost and error filtering are displaced to an offline stage. The experiments span several modalities but remain benchmark tasks with controlled execution. Retrieval errors, unsafe generated code, and tool-version drift are unresolved deployment risks. Budget limits also restrict the mathematical evaluation.

### Relevance to the survey

CRAFT is useful evidence for learned, task-specific tool ecosystems. In web extraction, generated tools could encode repeated navigation or transformation routines, but provenance, sandboxing, and revalidation are essential.
