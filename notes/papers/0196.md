## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

JARVIS-1 targets broad, long-horizon control in Minecraft using human-like visual observations and keyboard/mouse actions. It combines a multimodal language-model planner with goal-conditioned controllers and a multimodal memory holding both pretrained knowledge and experience acquired during play.

### Method and evaluation

Visual observations and instructions are mapped to plans, relevant memory is retrieved to improve those plans, and controllers execute skills through the native interface. The system is evaluated on more than 200 tasks from the Minecraft Universe Benchmark, ranging from short actions to obtaining a diamond pickaxe. Short-horizon tasks approach ceiling performance. On the highlighted long-horizon task, memory and experience improve success from about 6.2% to 12.5%, and the authors report up to a fivefold advantage over VPT under their comparison. Ablations separate memory retrieval, planning, and controller effects.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Absolute success on the hardest task remains low, and the agent depends on trained controllers and Minecraft-specific experience. Planner, retriever, and controller failures are coupled, making end-to-end attribution difficult. A large task count also does not imply unconstrained open-world competence when tasks share the same environment and item ontology.

### Relevance to the survey

JARVIS-1 is strong supporting evidence for multimodal memory in long-horizon agents. For web automation, its lesson is architectural: plans should retrieve prior visual/action experience, but reported breadth must be separated from reliability on genuinely new sites.
