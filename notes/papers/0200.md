## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

Self-Discover lets an LLM construct a task-specific reasoning scaffold instead of imposing one fixed prompting strategy. At task level, the model selects potentially useful reasoning modules, adapts them to the problem, and composes them into a structured plan; at instance level, it fills that structure to produce an answer.

### Method and evaluation

The three discovery operations are SELECT, ADAPT, and IMPLEMENT, seeded by general reasoning modules written in natural language. The resulting key-value structure is reused for every instance of a task, amortizing discovery cost. Evaluation with PaLM 2-L and GPT-4 spans 23 BigBench-Hard tasks, Thinking for Doing grounded-agent reasoning, and MATH, with direct answer, chain-of-thought, Plan-and-Solve, and self-consistency baselines. Self-Discover improves aggregate BBH accuracy over chain-of-thought by seven absolute points for PaLM 2-L and six for GPT-4, and reports larger gains on selected difficult tasks. Transfer experiments show that structures discovered by one model can help another, while ablations indicate that composing modules matters beyond simply listing them.

### Limitations and critical assessment

The work evaluates static question answering and text-described grounded reasoning, not closed-loop interaction with environmental feedback. The discovery prompts and seed module set are human designed, and the paper does not show that the learned structures are minimal, faithful explanations of internal reasoning, or robust under task distribution shift. Gains vary by task and are smaller on MATH; error analysis finds that even valid structures are often executed incorrectly. Comparisons with search-heavy methods emphasize inference cost but do not normalize all engineering and task-level discovery costs. The reported success therefore supports adaptive prompting, not autonomous acquisition of executable agent policies.

### Relevance to the survey

The paper is relevant to planning decomposition and reusable reasoning templates. It suggests that an agent can select a structured reasoning process per task rather than apply chain-of-thought uniformly, but web-agent use would still need grounding, state validation, and recovery after real actions.
