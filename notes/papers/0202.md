## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

ReadAgent addresses contexts longer than a model can reliably consume. It imitates active human reading through three operations: segment a document into episodes, compress each episode into a short “gist” memory, and selectively reopen original pages when a question requires detail.

### Method and evaluation

The model itself decides episode boundaries and produces gist memories. At question time it selects relevant gists, retrieves the associated original pages, and answers from the refreshed evidence. Evaluation covers QuALITY, NarrativeQA, and QMSum against direct long-context, retrieval, and gist-only baselines. The system improves all three reported tasks while extending effective context by roughly 3.5–20×. On QuALITY, a lookup variant reaches 87.17% accuracy in the reported setting; the paper also analyzes lookup count, token savings, prompting, and a MemWalker reimplementation.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Gists can omit decisive details, and lookup is only as good as the model's relevance judgment. The method uses extra sequential calls, so context savings do not equal lower latency or cost. The tasks are static documents; changing web pages require timestamped memories and invalidation. Evaluation also depends on strong proprietary models.

### Relevance to the survey

ReadAgent is a concrete design for hierarchical memory in long-page or multi-page web tasks: retain compact navigation-level summaries, then reopen the source before making a claim.
