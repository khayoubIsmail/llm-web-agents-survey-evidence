## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

This paper tests when iterative correction or tree search is actually preferable to simple candidate reranking. It factors an agent into generator, discriminator, and planner, then studies how discriminator accuracy changes the value of more elaborate planning.

### Method and evaluation

Experiments use text-to-SQL and mathematical reasoning. Real LLM discriminators are supplemented with simulated discriminators whose accuracy can be controlled, allowing the authors to estimate a threshold rather than merely compare end systems. Advanced methods require roughly 90% discrimination accuracy for meaningful gains over reranking, while current LLM discriminators fall short. Tree search is reported to be 10–20 times slower in the studied configuration while producing negligible improvement with realistic discriminators.

### Limitations and critical assessment

The threshold is conditional on two tasks, the tested generators, search parameters, and program-oriented correctness checks. Other environments may provide denser feedback or better learned value functions. Still, the causal manipulation of discriminator quality is stronger evidence than a benchmark-only comparison and directly challenges the assumption that more search is automatically better.

### Relevance to the survey

Use as a qualification on agent planning claims: search depth is useful only when intermediate evaluation is accurate enough to direct it, and efficiency must be reported alongside success.
