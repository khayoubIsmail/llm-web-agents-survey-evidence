## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

360°REA argues that same-model self-reflection does not create durable team improvement unless feedback is converted into reusable experience. Inspired by organizational review, it builds a hierarchical multi-agent assessment process and maintains both individual- and team-level experience pools.

### Method and evaluation

Agents assess outputs from multiple perspectives, turn strengths and weaknesses into reusable guidance, and retrieve that experience on later tasks. The hierarchy separates task execution from evaluation and experience management. Experiments cover several complex reasoning/generation datasets and compare with single-pass, self-reflection, and multi-agent baselines. Ablations show that removing assessment or experience components reduces instruction-following and overall quality; the paper reports a 5.9% decrease on one instruction metric when a key element is removed.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Inputs are text-only, evaluation partly relies on LLM judgments, and agents based on the same model may share blind spots despite nominally different roles. Experience accumulation can preserve errors unless entries are independently checked and aged out. Cross-task reuse is promising but tested within a bounded benchmark distribution.

### Relevance to the survey

The work is relevant to long-running multi-agent web systems: feedback should become versioned, retrievable experience, but only after evidence-based validation and with mechanisms for correction or deletion.
