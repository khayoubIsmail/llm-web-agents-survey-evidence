## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

This review seeks a common vocabulary for three agent paradigms—tool use (including RAG), planning, and feedback learning. It defines task/environment context, three LLM-profiled roles (policy, evaluator, and dynamics model), and recurring task-agnostic workflows so implementations can be compared across paradigm-specific terminology.

### Method and findings

The paper is a conceptual synthesis of representative frameworks. It maps tool-use workflows from single-call tools through autonomous selection, planning workflows from direct generation to iterative and search-based schemes, and feedback-learning workflows according to where and how evaluation changes behavior. The role/workflow distinction is useful: the same LLM can implement policy, evaluation, or environment prediction, but those functions impose different evidence and output requirements.

The review identifies three design gaps: unifying base and autonomous tool workflows, developing universal tool-use workflows, and reconciling the different feedback forms needed by free-form revision versus tree/graph search. These are research directions, not experimentally validated solutions.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Selection and coding procedures are not a systematic-review protocol, and the fast-moving corpus makes coverage temporary. The abstraction can hide domain-specific constraints such as irreversible web actions, permissions, or visual grounding. Its contribution is taxonomic clarity rather than comparative effectiveness.

### Relevance to the survey

Use the LLM-profiled roles to describe where reasoning occurs in a web-agent pipeline and to prevent “agent” from collapsing policy, critic, and world-model functions into one label.
