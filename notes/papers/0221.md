## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

Chain-of-Agents (CoA) addresses long inputs without discarding chunks or placing the entire document in one context. Worker agents read sequential segments and pass forward an evolving message; a manager then synthesizes the final response. This interleaves reading and reasoning while keeping each call short.

### Method and evaluation

The framework is training-free and evaluated on nine long-context datasets covering multi-hop and long-document QA, query-focused and generic summarization, book summarization, and code completion. Baselines include RAG, full-context prompting, and other multi-agent designs. CoA improves the reported aggregate by up to 10%, with larger gains when retrieval misses gold evidence or when a long-context model encounters longer samples. An ablation confirms that both worker aggregation and the final manager matter.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Sequential communication can corrupt or omit early evidence, and every worker adds latency. The reported “cost-effective” description is relative to tested baselines, not cost-free; routing to smaller workers is left for future work. The system also assumes a fixed segmentation order and does not explore richer debate or backtracking.

### Relevance to the survey

CoA is directly relevant to multi-page web reading. It shows one way to cover every segment while avoiding an oversized prompt, but final claims should still link back to original pages rather than only the propagated worker message.
