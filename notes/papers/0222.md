## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

FlowBench formalizes workflow-guided agent planning, where external procedural knowledge is available but may be expressed as text, lists, code-like constraints, or flowcharts. It contributes 51 scenarios across six domains and a multi-tier evaluation of turn-level actions and session-level completion.

### Method and evaluation

The benchmark renders the same underlying workflow in multiple formats and tests whether agents follow it during dialogue and tool invocation. Metrics cover workflow consistency, helpfulness, human-like responses, tool choice, arguments, and end-to-end success; cross-scenario conditions also insert potentially distracting workflows. Results show that workflow knowledge usually helps but that performance depends on representation and task. Differences of roughly 5–7 F1 points between formats appear in some tool-invocation comparisons, while domain success remains low for several model/settings.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Many quality scores use an LLM judge, and the scenarios are constructed rather than live operational workflows. Formatting effects may partly reflect prompt familiarity. A supplied workflow can also be wrong or stale; the benchmark primarily tests compliance, not whether an agent should challenge the procedure.

### Relevance to the survey

FlowBench supports evaluating process adherence separately from final success. For web agents, workflow provenance, versioning, and conflict handling should accompany any claim that external procedures reduce hallucination.
