## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

Planning in the Dark reduces expert dependence in hybrid LLM–symbolic planning. Instead of accepting one LLM-generated action schema, it builds a library of alternative schemas, uses semantic validation/ranking, and sends viable candidates to a symbolic planner.

### Method and evaluation

Natural-language domain descriptions generate multiple PDDL-style schemas. A learned semantic matcher ranks schema–description consistency, while planning feasibility filters candidates; returned plans can then be ranked for the user. Experiments compare direct LLM plans, tree-of-thought, and variants of the proposed pipeline across natural-language planning domains, supplemented by expert ratings of plan quality. The multi-candidate approach improves robustness to ambiguous descriptions and avoids committing to a single expert or model interpretation.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Candidate generation and combinatorial validation are expensive, solvability is not semantic correctness, and the matcher inherits training-distribution bias. The deterministic PDDL setting omits stochastic and hidden web state. The paper's own long-horizon illustration is important: even 99% per-step correctness yields only about 36.6% probability of a fully correct 100-step plan.

### Relevance to the survey

Use for hybrid planning and ambiguity management. Web-agent plans should be executable and may need multiple interpretations, but candidate diversity must be paired with semantic checks against live state.
