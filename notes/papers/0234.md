## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

AgentBank tests whether scaling diverse interaction trajectories can produce a more general open-source agent. The dataset contains more than 50,000 trajectories from 16 tasks spanning five skill dimensions, and the resulting 7B/13B models are named Samoyed.

### Method and evaluation

An annotation pipeline collects teacher-agent trajectories while reducing the tendency to retain only easy successes. Training uses complete instruction–action–observation sequences but computes loss only on agent responses. Comparisons involve Llama-2-Chat, CodeLlama, Mistral, and Llama-3 backbones; AgentLM and Agent-FLAN are principal tuned baselines, with GPT-3.5/4 as reference points. Evaluation covers held-in tasks and unseen held-out tasks. Scaling trajectory count and task diversity improves both, while reducing the 50k+ corpus causes a clear fall in generalized capability.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Teacher-generated data can reproduce teacher errors and stylistic bias. The primary 7B/13B study cannot show whether the scaling trend persists at larger sizes, and “held-out” tasks remain related to the collection's agent distributions. Training on trajectories also optimizes imitation, not necessarily recovery from novel failures.

### Relevance to the survey

AgentBank is evidence that diversity and scale of interaction data matter for transferable agent skills. Web-agent datasets should report task, site, and trajectory diversity rather than only trajectory count.
