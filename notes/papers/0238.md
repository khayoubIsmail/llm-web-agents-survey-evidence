## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

ProactiveBench studies agents that offer help without an explicit request. Human activity traces are converted into candidate proactive tasks, annotators accept or reject them, and a learned reward model approximates those judgments. The final dataset contains 6,790 events.

### Method and evaluation

The reward model is trained from 1,640 labeled examples and tested on 120, while multiple LLMs are evaluated on whether and when they should propose assistance. Fine-tuning on ProactiveBench raises Qwen2-7B-Instruct to 66.47% F1, above the tested open and closed models. The reward model reaches 91.80% F1 against human labels in the stated test; annotator agreement is 91.67%. Detailed analysis separates missed-needed help, correct silence, correct assistance, and false alarms.

### Limitations and critical assessment

False alarms remain frequent and can harm trust even when aggregate F1 improves. Contexts cover limited environments, and a reward model trained on a small labeled set can encode annotator preferences as universal norms. Proactive action also raises consent and safety concerns beyond task prediction.

### Relevance to the survey

Use for proactive web agents, with precision and interruption cost emphasized alongside recall. Systems should seek confirmation before irreversible actions even when a proactive suggestion is appropriate.
