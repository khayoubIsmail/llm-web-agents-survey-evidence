## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

Critique-Guided Improvement (CGI) separates an actor that explores from a critic trained to produce actionable natural-language feedback. The actor is then refined on trajectories paired with critiques it followed successfully, creating an iterative two-player training loop.

### Method and evaluation

Experiments use ScienceWorld, WebShop, and TextCraft. A critique-generation stage trains a small critic from fine-grained assessments; three action-refinement iterations then update the actor. Comparisons include no critique, numerical discriminators, self-refinement, supervised fine-tuning, and GPT-4 feedback. With Llama-3-8B, critiques yield a reported 42.89% average improvement over no critique, and the final method exceeds SFT by 15.99% in the highlighted aggregate. Hard tasks gain most after repeated refinement.

### Limitations and critical assessment

The pipeline uses thousands of generated critique/action examples and eight A100 GPUs for reported fine-tuning, so efficiency claims need lifecycle accounting. A critic can provide fluent but incorrect guidance, and the three environments have relatively clear feedback. The “small critic surpasses GPT-4” statement is specific to the paper's feedback metric, not general model quality.

### Relevance to the survey

CGI supports using textual feedback when scalar rewards cannot explain a failure. Web agents should retain the evidence behind each critique and measure whether revisions actually fix execution.
