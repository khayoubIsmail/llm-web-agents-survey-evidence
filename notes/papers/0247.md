## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

This paper isolates three decision failures in LLM agents: greedy exploitation, frequency bias toward commonly sampled actions, and a knowing–doing gap between stated knowledge and behavior. It then tests reinforcement-learning fine-tuning (RLFT) on self-generated chain-of-thought rationales and explicit exploration mechanisms.

### Method and evaluation

Gemma-2 models at 2B, 9B, and 27B are evaluated on textual multi-armed bandits, contextual bandits, and tic-tac-toe. Experiments compare base behavior, RLFT on self-generated or expert rationales, reward shaping, epsilon-greedy exploration, self-correction, and self-consistency. RLFT broadens action coverage and reduces frequency bias; one analysis lowers the dominant action share from 70% to 35%, while another raises exploration from 50% to 70%. Models can act near-optimally when enough information is supplied, supporting the knowing–doing diagnosis.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Fine-tuned LLMs remain worse than dedicated bandit algorithms. Tests focus on Gemma-2 and a 50-step horizon that is insufficient for the largest 20-arm setting. Bandits deliberately simplify state and delayed consequences, so improved exploration may not transfer to goal-directed web tasks.

### Relevance to the survey

Use to distinguish verbal reasoning competence from exploration policy. Web-agent evaluations should measure action diversity, regret, and recovery rather than infer decision quality from chain-of-thought.
