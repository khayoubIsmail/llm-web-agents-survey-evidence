---

#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

- **Decomposition:** ReachAgent separates getting to the correct page from operating on that page, isolating navigation versus local GUI manipulation.
- **Step-level gains:** the reported model improves **IoU by 7.12 points** and **text accuracy by 7.69 points** over the compared state of the art.
- **Task-level gains:** overall metrics improve by roughly **4.72 and 4.63 points** on the corresponding task-level measures.
- **Learning signal:** preference/reward modeling is used to distinguish better GUI trajectories rather than only imitate demonstrations.
- **Survey implication:** explicitly modeling page reachability can reduce the tendency to conflate navigation and operation errors in mobile agents.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

Results remain benchmark/app dependent and the two-stage decomposition can propagate a wrong-page decision into operation failure. Mobile interfaces also change frequently across app versions.

---

#### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** It is mobile-specific, not a general web-browser agent.
- **Limitation 2:** Training data and action alignment may depend on mobile app structure.
- **Limitation 3:** Page reaching/operation helps but does not solve all long-horizon planning failures.

General thesis-level limitation:

```text
Improving one agent component is not enough for generalized web automation.
A robust web agent still needs grounding, planning, execution, verification, recovery, and safety.
```
