#### Key finding / main claim

The field is shifting from specialized agents toward foundation-model-based agents, from text to image observations, and toward behavior cloning, while generalization and planning remain open problems.

For S4, the important question is not only whether the method works on its benchmark, but **which part of web/GUI agency it improves**:

- perception and grounding,
- planning and reasoning,
- action execution,
- API/tool use,
- memory/state tracking,
- recovery and verification,
- or deployment/evaluation.

---

#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

- **Evolution:** the survey documents the move from specialized agents toward foundation-model computer-use agents and from mostly text/structure observations toward increasingly visual/hybrid interaction.
- **Training trend:** behavior cloning remains dominant even as reinforcement/self-improvement methods expand.
- **Six major gaps:** generalization, learning efficiency, planning, benchmark task complexity, standardized evaluation and the research–practice disconnect.
- **Benchmark warning:** heterogeneity in environments/action spaces makes direct leaderboard comparison difficult.
- **Survey role:** it provides peer-reviewed synthesis for positioning computer-use agents as a broader class containing Web, desktop and mobile agents.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

The paper is a literature synthesis rather than new controlled evidence; rapidly changing agent products and model releases mean quantitative rankings age quickly.
