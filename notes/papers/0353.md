#### Thesis-ready paragraph

BEARCUBS: A benchmark for computer-using web agents extends the benchmark landscape for LLM-based web agents by focusing on **live-web information seeking with multimodal interactions**. Its central contribution is that it evaluates agent behavior under conditions that are underrepresented in the core S5.1 P0 benchmarks. Specifically, it introduces or analyzes the setting: **111 information-seeking questions with short answers and human-validated trajectories.**. This matters for the thesis because generalized web automation and structured data extraction cannot be evaluated only by a single success score or by a single environment type. A robust web agent must generalize across websites, handle dynamic or multimodal content, maintain long-horizon context, avoid unsafe actions, and produce verifiable outputs. The paper's main finding is that **Humans score 84.7%; the best tested system in the original arXiv abstract reaches 24.3%, showing a large gap.**. However, its limitation is equally important: **Small benchmark; information-seeking focus rather than general state-changing automation.**. Therefore, the paper should be used as a P1 support reference that sharpens the S5.1 benchmark taxonomy and motivates later technical sections on grounding, planning, training, failure modes, safety, and deployment.

```text
Can an LLM-based agent operate reliably across diverse, dynamic, realistic websites

```

This benchmark contributes one important evaluation pressure:

```text
Live-web information seeking with multimodal interactions
```

For generalized web automation, this matters because benchmark design determines which failures become visible. A benchmark that only checks final answer accuracy may hide trajectory errors. A benchmark that only uses static pages may hide live-web brittleness. A benchmark that only tests text may hide visual grounding failures. A benchmark that ignores safety may overestimate deployment readiness.

#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

- **Benchmark:** BEARCUBS contains **111 live-Web information-seeking questions** with short unambiguous answers and human-validated browsing trajectories.
- **Multimodal requirement:** tasks deliberately include interactions such as **video understanding and 3D navigation** that cannot be bypassed by text-only scraping.
- **Human result:** **84.7% accuracy**, with search inefficiency and domain-knowledge gaps explaining many human misses.
- **Agent result:** the strongest reported computer-use system, OpenAI Operator, reaches only **24.3% accuracy**.
- **Maintenance:** the benchmark is designed to replace invalid/contaminated questions over time, acknowledging live-Web drift explicitly.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

The benchmark is intentionally small and information-seeking focused. Live content creates reproducibility and temporal-validity challenges, and changing questions complicate longitudinal comparison unless benchmark versions are recorded.
