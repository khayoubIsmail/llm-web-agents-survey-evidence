#### Thesis-ready paragraph

AgenticShop: Benchmarking Agentic Product Curation for Personalized Web Shopping extends the benchmark landscape for LLM-based web agents by focusing on **personalized web shopping benchmark**. Its central contribution is that it evaluates agent behavior under conditions that are underrepresented in the core S5.1 P0 benchmarks. Specifically, it introduces or analyzes the setting: **Personalized product curation tasks in open-web shopping settings.**. This matters for the thesis because generalized web automation and structured data extraction cannot be evaluated only by a single success score or by a single environment type. A robust web agent must generalize across websites, handle dynamic or multimodal content, maintain long-horizon context, avoid unsafe actions, and produce verifiable outputs. The paper's main finding is that **Current agentic systems remain insufficient for personalized product curation, with hallucinations and irrelevant product references.**. However, its limitation is equally important: **Domain-specific and preference-heavy; evaluation depends on checklists that may not capture all user preferences.**. Therefore, the paper should be used as a P1 support reference that sharpens the S5.1 benchmark taxonomy and motivates later technical sections on grounding, planning, training, failure modes, safety, and deployment.

#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

- **Problem:** AgenticShop evaluates **personalized product curation on the open Web**, going beyond single-platform product lookup.
- **Personalization:** tasks combine realistic shopping intents with user profiles/purchase histories and fine-grained preferences.
- **Evaluation:** a **checklist-driven, verifiable** framework checks whether recommended products actually satisfy requested/personalized properties rather than rewarding fluent recommendations.
- **Empirical finding:** current Web agents remain substantially insufficient and vary sharply by preference type/domain; price/review constraints and dynamic or inconsistent product information are frequent sources of error.
- **Survey role:** the benchmark links Web navigation to evidence-grounded multi-record product selection, making it especially relevant to schema/constraint completeness.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

Open-Web product pages change rapidly and commercial information can be inconsistent or inaccessible. Shopping-specific preference checklists do not generalize directly to every extraction schema or transactional domain.
