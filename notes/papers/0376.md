#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

- **Setting:** tau-bench evaluates agents that must simultaneously converse with an LM-simulated user, call domain APIs and follow written business policies.
- **Evaluation:** final database state is compared with the annotated goal state, avoiding dependence on one reference conversation.
- **Reliability metric:** **pass^k** measures whether an agent succeeds repeatedly rather than once by chance.
- **Result:** frontier function-calling systems such as GPT-4o complete **less than 50%** of tasks in the original study.
- **Consistency:** retail **pass^8 falls below 25%**, showing that one-run success substantially overstates reliable deployment performance.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

Users are model-simulated rather than real, and the original benchmark covers only selected enterprise domains. pass^k exposes stochastic reliability but does not by itself identify why a run violated policy or failed.

---

#### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** Domain coverage is limited compared with the whole web.
- **Limitation 2:** Performance can depend strongly on the simulated user and policy design.
- **Limitation 3:** Tool environments are structured; arbitrary websites are messier.

General limitation for your thesis:

```text
A benchmark evaluates only a slice of agent behavior.
No single benchmark proves that an agent can perform generalized web automation and data extraction.
```

Therefore, use this paper as a **P2 supporting source**, not as the sole basis for a central claim.
