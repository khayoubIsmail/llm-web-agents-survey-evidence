#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

- **Benchmark:** CASA evaluates cultural/social awareness in two Web-agent contexts: **online shopping and social discussion forums**.
- **Metrics:** awareness coverage, helpfulness and violation rate distinguish recognizing a problematic situation from actually behaving safely.
- **Result:** Web-agent settings produce **less than 10% awareness coverage** and **more than 40% violation rates** in the reported experiments, much worse than non-agent language settings.
- **Mitigation:** culture-specific fine-tuning improves cross-region generalization while prompting helps navigation/task handling; combining them provides complementary gains.
- **Implication:** adding browser autonomy can degrade social/normative behavior even when the underlying model appears aware in static prompts.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

Cultural norms are context-dependent and cannot be exhaustively represented by a fixed benchmark. Automated norm labels risk oversimplification, and the two Web domains do not cover all high-stakes settings.

---

#### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** Web environments are dynamic and hard to reproduce.
- **Limitation 2:** LLM-based or trajectory-based evaluation can be noisy.
- **Limitation 3:** Strong results may depend on specific scaffolds, browsers, websites, or task selection.
