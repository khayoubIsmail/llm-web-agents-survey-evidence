#### Key finding / main claim

HTML preserves structural and semantic information that is lost in plain text conversion, and experiments on six QA datasets show advantages for HTML-based RAG.

---

#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

HtmlRAG keeps retrieved webpages in HTML rather than flattening them to plain text, then removes noise and applies block-tree pruning to fit the context budget. Across ASQA, HotpotQA, NQ, TriviaQA, MuSiQue and ELI5, HTML-based pruning is consistently competitive with or better than text/chunk baselines; for example, the released Llama-pruner results reach 60.75 EM on NQ and 93.0 on TriviaQA, while the Phi pruner reaches 46.25 on HotpotQA. The study directly supports preserving webpage structure for retrieval and extraction-oriented reasoning.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

HTML introduces additional tags/noise and pruning can discard evidence. The paper addresses retrieval context modeling, not interactive browser action or end-to-end provenance verification.

---

#### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** HTML is longer and noisier than plain text.
- **Limitation 2:** Cleaning and pruning can remove useful information if not tuned carefully.
- **Limitation 3:** It is RAG-focused, not a direct web-action agent paper.

General thesis-level limitation:

```text
Better perception or representation is necessary but not sufficient.
A generalized web agent also needs planning, memory, action execution, error recovery, and evaluation.
```
