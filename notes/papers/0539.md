#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

Tree-Guided Preference Optimization (TGPO) addresses sparse rewards and credit-assignment conflicts by merging semantically equivalent states from different trajectories into a tree and deriving consistent local preference relationships from the shared structure. This avoids contradictory labels that arise when the same intermediate state appears inside globally successful and unsuccessful rollouts. The paper's web-agent experiments show more robust offline preference optimization than flat trajectory-level preference construction.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

Semantic state merging is itself approximate: incorrectly merging distinct states or failing to merge equivalent states can corrupt preference labels. Offline optimization also cannot discover behaviors absent from the collected trajectory tree.
