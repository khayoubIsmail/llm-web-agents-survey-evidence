## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

AutoGuide converts offline agent experience into concise, conditional natural-language guidelines. Instead of retrieving a whole successful trajectory, it contrasts outcomes to extract what should or should not be done in a particular state, then retrieves only guidelines applicable to the current context.

### Method and evaluation

Offline trajectories are grouped and compared to identify decisions associated with success or failure. A generator writes state-aware “if context, then guidance” rules, and a selector adds a small relevant subset to the agent prompt. Tests cover ALFWorld, WebShop, and WebArena against ReAct, demonstration retrieval, reflection, and static-guideline baselines. In the difficult WebArena setting, ReAct records 8.0% task success and AutoGuide improves the reported result by 47.1% relative in the highlighted comparison. Ablations examine context conditions, contrastive extraction, and selection.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Guideline quality is bounded by the coverage and correctness of offline data. Natural-language rules can conflict, overgeneralize, or become stale after a site change; selection errors then surface irrelevant advice. Extraction also uses powerful LLMs and shifts cost offline. The gains do not eliminate low absolute success on realistic web tasks.

### Relevance to the survey

AutoGuide is useful evidence for distilling trajectories into inspectable policy memory. Web deployments should version each guideline, retain its supporting episodes, and allow invalidation.
