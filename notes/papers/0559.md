## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

Exploration-based Trajectory Optimization (ETO) trains an agent from its own failures instead of using only successful expert demonstrations. After behavior cloning, the agent explores; failed and successful trajectories for the same task form preference pairs used for DPO, and the cycle repeats.

### Method and evaluation

Experiments use WebShop, ScienceWorld, and ALFWorld, with seen/unseen splits and trajectories averaging roughly 5–14 turns. Baselines include direct LLMs, SFT, best-of-N, and other preference/RL approaches. ETO improves both reward and efficiency across environments; the paper highlights a 22% improvement over SFT on the ScienceWorld out-of-distribution split and average reward gains around 8–9.5% in selected comparisons. Iterative exploration supplies harder, policy-relevant negatives that static expert data lacks.

### Limitations and critical assessment

ETO assumes a successful trajectory is available for comparison or that self-play eventually discovers one. Pairwise final reward may not reveal which step caused failure, and repeated exploration can be expensive or unsafe outside simulators. DPO optimizes relative preference and does not guarantee calibrated action values.

### Relevance to the survey

Use for learning from failed web trajectories. Deployment should collect failures in a sandbox, identify causal steps when possible, and avoid exploring irreversible actions on live accounts.
