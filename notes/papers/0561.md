## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

Navigating WebAI examines supervised and reinforcement learning for MiniWoB++ while probing whether agents understand HTML structure or memorize target elements. It proposes behavioral-cloning and hierarchical-planning variants and experiments with adding RL after supervised training.

### Method and evaluation

T5-base and T5-large models are trained from substantially less data than earlier web-navigation systems, with inputs including HTML, instruction, and action history. T5-large reaches about 43.5–43.6% average accuracy in the principal supervised/hierarchical result. A hybrid with a CC-Net-style RL component falls from 36.69% after supervised training to 33.86% after RL, attributed to covariate shift rather than an assumed automatic RL benefit. Removing action history causes a roughly 14% decrease in the cited ablation.

### Limitations and critical assessment

MiniWoB++ is narrow and template-like, and accuracy does not fully measure successful multi-step execution. The study itself finds memorization and poor transfer; small models remain constrained, while larger pretrained models still lead. The unsuccessful RL phase is useful negative evidence that combining paradigms without distribution control can hurt.

### Relevance to the survey

Use as a caution against reporting training recipes without ablations. Web agents need tests that distinguish structural understanding from memorized selectors and should report negative RL results.
