## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

AgentGym is a unified interactive framework for evaluating and training agents across seven real-world scenarios, 14 environments, and 89 tasks. It supplies expanded instructions, trajectories, concurrent environment interfaces, and a benchmark rather than only aggregating static datasets.

### Method and evaluation

Environments span web navigation, text games, household tasks, digital games, tool use, and programming. The framework supports live feedback, exploration, SFT on AgentTraj, and an AgentSTaR self-improvement procedure. Comparisons include direct proprietary/open models and exploration/training baselines. Trajectory-tuned agents approach commercial-model results on selected aggregate measures; the displayed ablation includes task scores of 66.5, 77.5, 69.3, and 44.0 for one AgentTraj-SFT configuration.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Unifying interfaces does not equalize task difficulty, action semantics, or success metrics. Training and evaluation in the same framework may introduce implementation-specific advantages, and several environments are simulations. The breadth is valuable infrastructure evidence, but claims of general capability require held-out environments, not only held-out instructions.

### Relevance to the survey

AgentGym supports cross-environment evaluation and training. Use it to argue for common interfaces plus scenario-specific metrics, while preserving environment-level results rather than a single aggregate.
