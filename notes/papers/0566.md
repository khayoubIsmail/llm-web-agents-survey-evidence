## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

Synatra turns indirect procedural knowledge—such as tutorials and static web content—into direct action demonstrations. It defines types of indirect knowledge, synthesizes tasks and trajectories around page observations, and filters/structures them for agent fine-tuning.

### Method and evaluation

The pipeline produces 99,920 web-navigation examples from WikiHow and ClueWeb, then fine-tunes CodeLlama-7B. Evaluation uses Mind2Web, MiniWoB++, and WebArena. The model improves from 6.62% to 15.85% on Mind2Web and from 23.04% to 38.20% on MiniWoB++ relative to the stated CodeLlama-Instruct baseline, and surpasses comparably sized models plus GPT-3.5 on selected WebArena/Mind2Web comparisons. Synthetic examples are estimated at $0.031 each—about 3% of the human-demonstration cost used by the authors—and scaling analyses show continued benefit from more data.

### Limitations and critical assessment

Synthesis depends on a strong model and the quality of source pages; tutorials may be stale, incomplete, or describe actions not executable in the observed interface. Some evaluation setups provide accessibility trees containing the ground-truth element, which narrows the full grounding problem. Cost comparisons omit all validation and infrastructure choices.

### Relevance to the survey

Synatra is strong evidence for using abundant web instructions as weak supervision, provided synthesized trajectories are executed, filtered, and provenance-linked before training.
