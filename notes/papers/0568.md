## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

OS-Genesis reverses the usual GUI-data pipeline: an agent first explores a real interface step by step, low-level actions are annotated, and a high-level task is synthesized retrospectively from the observed trajectory. A trajectory reward model then scores completion and coherence so incomplete but useful explorations need not be discarded.

### Method and evaluation

GPT-4o performs environment exploration, task annotation, and trajectory reward modeling. Reward-weighted trajectories train InternVL2-4B/8B and Qwen2-VL-7B agents with planning and action objectives. On AndroidWorld, AndroidControl, and WebArena, OS-Genesis is compared with zero-shot, task-driven, and self-instruction data generation. For Qwen2-VL-7B, AndroidWorld success rises from 9.82% with task-driven self-instruction to 17.41% with OS-Genesis; on WebArena its success is 10.79% versus 5.39% for that baseline. Diversity analyses use embedding distance, reward-model ablations test weighting versus filtering, scaling experiments show eventual saturation, and equal-size comparisons indicate that OS-Genesis retains more than 80% of the performance obtained from human trajectories.

### Limitations and critical assessment

The pipeline depends on proprietary GPT-4o both to act and to judge data, coupling exploration coverage and quality control to one model family. Retrospective tasks are guaranteed to fit observed actions but may favor easy or accidental behaviors and need not reflect user demand. Reward-weighted sampling cannot correct systematic judge errors, and embedding distance is only a proxy for semantic task diversity. Training and evaluation use virtualized environments; real devices introduce permissions, irreversible actions, authentication, and privacy risks. The authors also report saturation from model capacity and exploration limits, and do not test text-only or vision-only trajectory variants.

### Relevance to the survey

OS-Genesis is evidence for environment-grounded synthetic trajectory generation and for using incomplete interaction traces rather than only successful demonstrations. It is directly relevant to web/GUI agent training, but deployment requires independent trajectory validation and tightly sandboxed exploration.
