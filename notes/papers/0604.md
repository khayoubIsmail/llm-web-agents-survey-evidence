## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->
### Research problem and contribution

WebSynthesis avoids live-site infrastructure limits and irreversible actions by training an LLM world model to simulate web transitions, then running MCTS over that model to generate diverse trajectories offline.

### Method and evaluation

The world model learns UI dynamics from seed interactions. WebMCTS treats predicted states as reversible branches, uses reward/termination models to retain useful paths, and converts selected searches into training trajectories. Agents trained on these data are evaluated on WebVoyager, Mind2Web-Online, and WebArena-Lite against GPT-4, AgentTrek, and OS-Genesis. WebSynthesis-14B reaches 39.29% average on WebVoyager, 31.82% on Mind2Web-Online, and 17.28%/28.34% Pass@1/Pass@3 in the displayed WebArena-Lite comparison. Ablations show that learned dynamics plus MCTS outperform prompt-only generation and beam variants.

### Limitations and critical assessment

The world model covers limited seed websites and performs worse on complex accessibility trees. Simulated trajectories can exploit model errors and omit real network, authentication, and anti-bot failures. Evaluation covers general navigation rather than sensitive or highly specialized tasks. The approach replaces some live interaction but does not remove the need for real-world validation.

### Relevance to the survey

Use as evidence for model-based synthetic web data and reversible offline search. A safe pipeline should measure simulator fidelity and test every learned policy on held-out live state.
