#### Simple understanding

This paper studies automatic evaluators for digital agents. Instead of relying only on hand-written success functions or human judgment, it uses domain-general VLM/LM evaluators to judge agent trajectories and then uses those judgments to improve agents.

```text

Paper → This work studies, categorizes, evaluates, detects, or mitigates such failures.
Goal → Make agent behavior more reliable, diagnosable, safe, and robust.
```

---

#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

This paper treats automatic evaluation as an active agent-improvement component rather than a post-hoc metric. Its domain-general evaluators achieve 74.4%-92.9% agreement with oracle evaluation metrics across digital-agent benchmarks. Using evaluator feedback without additional external supervision improves state-of-the-art WebArena performance by 29% relatively and yields roughly 75% relative improvement in device-control settings through fine-tuning and inference-time guidance.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

Evaluator mistakes can become policy-training mistakes, and high oracle agreement does not imply calibrated feedback on rare failure states. More powerful evaluators also add inference cost and can make agent performance dependent on another model.

---

#### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** Evaluator quality is imperfect and can introduce false positive or false negative success judgments.
- **Limitation 2:** End-to-end VLM evaluation can be expensive and depends on proprietary models.
- **Limitation 3:** Evaluation from screenshots may miss hidden state or server-side effects.

General thesis-level limitation:

```text
Failure analysis is necessary but not sufficient.
A taxonomy or benchmark identifies the problem, but reliable deployment also needs mitigation, verification, monitoring, and safety constraints.
```

Therefore, this paper should normally be used as **P2 support** unless it becomes central to the S5.5 subsection.
