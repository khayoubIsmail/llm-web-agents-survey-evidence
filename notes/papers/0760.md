## Analysis

<!-- note-body-start -->

### Core contribution

Yu et al. introduce the **TrustAgent** survey framework for trustworthy LLM agents and multi-agent systems. The paper is important because it organizes trustworthiness through three simultaneous views rather than only enumerating attacks:

1. **Modular:** intrinsic brain/memory/tool components and extrinsic agent–agent, agent–environment, and agent–user interactions.
2. **Technical:** attacks, defenses, and evaluation.
3. **Trust dimensions:** safety, privacy, truthfulness, fairness, and robustness.

### Main findings and organization

- **Brain:** jailbreaks, prompt injection, backdoors, hallucination/truthfulness, alignment and filtering defenses, and targeted/general evaluation.
- **Memory:** poisoning, privacy leakage, misuse, detection/context/output defenses, and a noted lack of systematic memory-trust evaluation.
- **Tools:** manipulation and unsafe tool use, relatively immature defense coverage, sandbox/dataset evaluation, and risks from tool chains.
- **Agent–agent:** cooperative/infectious attack propagation, collaborative/topological defenses, and limited dedicated evaluation.
- **Agent–environment:** risks from physical and digital environments; environmental feedback can mislead agents, while evaluation remains domain fragmented.
- **Agent–user:** trust calibration, transparency, personalization, privacy, and human oversight.
- **Appendix A:** explicitly defines safety, privacy, truthfulness, fairness, and robustness.
- **Appendix B:** provides a comprehensive taxonomy mapping the surveyed literature into the framework.

### Relevance to this survey

The paper is a strong **Cluster D** comparator because it distinguishes a dimension-based trust decomposition from threat-oriented surveys. It also supports the manuscript's security and governance discussion by showing that trust boundaries span model internals, persistent memory, tools, other agents, environments, and users.

For generalized Web extraction, however, the paper stops short of an operational source-to-dataset integrity contract. It does not define field-level provenance, record association, schema coverage, or a benchmark for silent corruption of extracted values. This makes it useful both as prior work and as a boundary case for the manuscript's §8.4 argument.

### Limitations

- Broad LLM-agent/MAS survey rather than a Web/computer-use-specific survey.
- No new empirical Web-agent system or extraction benchmark.
- No extraction-specific evidence-object schema or provenance metric.
- No direct end-to-end evaluation of silent field/dataset corruption.
- Surveyed primary literature includes heterogeneous publication statuses, although this survey itself is archival KDD 2025.

### Synthesis statement

An archival KDD 2025 survey that decomposes LLM-agent trust across intrinsic/extrinsic modules, attack-defense-evaluation roles, and five explicit trust dimensions. It directly strengthens Cluster D and the manuscript's end-to-end trust discussion, but it does not operationalize extraction-specific field integrity or source-linked provenance.
