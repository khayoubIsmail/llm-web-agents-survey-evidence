#### Concrete evidence verified from the full paper

The Behavioral Fabric study tests how explicit user values/preferences and interface cues shape web-agent trajectories. The controlled testbed contains 14 interactive tasks across six replica shopping, travel, dining and housing sites, 12 value/preference personas, and four state-of-the-art agents. Persona guidance changes filter use and decision trajectories, while no-guidance agents show a strong shortest-path efficiency bias. Promotional UI cues such as discounts/bestseller badges can override value-consistent exploration: for GPT-4o, mean steps in value-conditioned tasks fall from 5.86 without promotion to 2.60 with promotion (t=6.13, p<.001, Hedges' g=-2.21), with similarly large effects for DeepSeek and Claude. Longer trajectories are significantly associated with better alignment (ANOVA F(2,424)=6.53, p=.002), while agents often rationalize promotion-driven choices as if they reflected the requested value.

##### Author-stated / evidence-based limitations

The authors primarily use Browser-use as the interaction framework, employ fixed promotional cues, and study a bounded set of replica sites/tasks. Results depend on prompt/persona construction; externalized reasoning is not direct evidence of latent cognition, and the chosen human-value framework is incomplete and culturally limited.

---

#### Limitations

- **Limitation 1:** Controlled testbed with replicated websites, not full open web.
- **Limitation 2:** Persona prompting may not capture all real user values.
- **Limitation 3:** Agent behavior can rationalize choices after being influenced by interface cues.
